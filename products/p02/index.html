How to use this pack

Kids walk farther and happier when they have a mission. Hand them a hunt sheet at the trailhead and the hike becomes a search, not a march.

Printing tips

Print at Actual size on US Letter. An A4 file and a low-ink printer-friendly file are included.

Card stock works well for bingo and the certificate. A clipboard and a crayon or pencil make the hunt easy on the move.

Reprint the hunts and journal pages as often as you like for your own family.

Spot it, don't take it

Every hunt is a looking game. Nothing gets picked, collected or taken home. Kids tick the box, then leave the leaf, cone or rock for the next family. Many parks do not allow collecting at all.

Before you go: choose a short trail the slowest walker can finish, check the weather and park alerts, and pack water, snacks and layers. Give each child a whistle and practice the trail rules on page 3 at home. Take a photo of your child at the trailhead so you know exactly what they are wearing today. Do a tick check after the hike.

My trail rules

Stay where you can see your grown-up

If you cannot see them, stop and call out. Grown-ups lead or follow, kids stay in the middle.

Stop at every trail sign and fork

Wait there for your group, even if you know the way. Pick the path together.

Stay on the trail

Trails keep you safe and protect plants. No shortcuts, even when it looks quicker.

Look, don't touch or taste

Never eat berries, mushrooms or plants from the trail. Leave animals and their homes alone.

Leaves of three, let it be

Poison ivy can have three shiny or dull leaves. Do not touch plants you do not know.

If you are lost, hug a tree

Stop. Stay in one place by a tree, even if you are scared. People will come to find you. Staying still makes you easier to find.

Blow your whistle three times

Three blows means "I need help." Wait, then blow three times again. When you hear someone call your name, shout back. You are not in trouble.

Practice at home: play "hug a tree" in the yard or a park. Your child picks a tree, stays put and blows the whistle while you walk over.

Leave No Trace for kids

Seven trail promises

Good explorers leave the trail just as nice as they found it, so animals stay healthy and the next family can enjoy it too.

PROMISE 1

Plan with your grown-up

Look at the map before you go. Pack water, a snack, a jacket and your whistle.

PROMISE 2

Stay on the path

Walk on the trail, even through puddles and mud, so plants on the sides can keep growing.

PROMISE 3

Pack out every crumb

Everything you bring comes home with you. Wrappers, apple cores and peels too.

PROMISE 4

Leave what you find

Rocks, cones, flowers and feathers stay on the trail. Take a picture or draw them instead.

PROMISE 5

Be careful with fire

Only grown-ups make fires, only where it is allowed. Never play with matches.

PROMISE 6

Watch animals from far away

Be quiet and still. Never feed wild animals, not even a tiny crumb. People food makes them sick.

PROMISE 7

Share the trail kindly

Say hi, step aside to let people pass, and use your quiet trail voice so everyone can hear the birds.

My promise

I will follow the seven trail promises on every hike.

Slow down and notice

Five senses walk

Find a safe spot beside the trail. Stop, breathe, and use one sense at a time. Write or draw what you notice.

5 things I can see

Look up, look down, look far away.

4 things I can hear

Close your eyes and count to ten first.

3 things I can touch

Bark, a smooth rock, cool water. Only safe things your grown-up says are OK.

2 things I can smell

Pine needles, wet soil, flowers, rain.

1 thing I can taste

Your trail snack! Never taste plants or berries from the trail.

Great for tired legs. A senses stop is a rest that feels like a game.

Kids Trail Explorer Pack18
Read the trail

Trail sign and blaze spotter

Trails talk to hikers with signs and marks. Learn what they mean, then keep a tally of every one you spot.

Trail blaze

A painted mark on a tree or rock. It means you are on the trail. Each trail has its own color or shape.

Tally

Turn ahead

Two blazes, one above the other, mean the trail turns soon. On many trails the top one leans the way to go.

Tally

Junction sign

Where trails meet. It shows trail names, arrows and how far to go. Stop here and wait for your group.

Tally

Trailhead map

The big board at the start. Find the dot that says You Are Here before you set off.

Tally

Rock pile (cairn)

Some trails over rock use piles of stones as markers. Never add rocks or knock them down.

Tally

Mile or km marker

A small post with a number. Some trails count miles, some count kilometers.

Tally

Draw the blaze on your trail today

My trail's blaze
Its color and shape
Good to know: blaze colors and shapes are different from park to park. Check the map at the trailhead to learn yours.
