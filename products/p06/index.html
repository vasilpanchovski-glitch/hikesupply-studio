How to use this guide

Yellowstone and Grand Teton sit next to each other in the northwest corner of Wyoming, joined by one road. Together they make one of the best first national park trips in North America: geysers, canyons, wildlife and some of the most famous mountain views anywhere.

Who it is for

First-time visitors and beginner to intermediate hikers. Every hike in this guide is a day hike on a marked trail, from a 20-minute walk to a half-day climb.

How to read the hikes

Distances are round trip, in miles and kilometers. Elevation gain is in feet and meters. Times are the ranges the National Park Service gives. Where the park lists no gain figure, we describe the climb in words instead of guessing.

The two parks

At a glance

Yellowstone is big and spread out: you drive between areas and take short walks at each stop. Grand Teton is smaller and built around one mountain range and a chain of lakes, so hikes start close together.

Yellowstone

SizeNearly 3,500 sq mi (9,000 km²), in Wyoming, Montana and Idaho
EntrancesFive: North, Northeast, East, South and West. Several hours of driving between them
Main roadThe Grand Loop, a figure eight of about 142 mi (229 km) linking the main areas
HeightMost of the park is above 7,000 ft (2,100 m)
Known forGeysers and hot springs, the Grand Canyon of the Yellowstone, bison, elk, wolves and bears

Grand Teton

SizeAbout 310,000 acres (1,250 km²), in Wyoming
EntrancesMoose and Granite Canyon in the south, Moran in the east, and from Yellowstone in the north
Main roadsTeton Park Road along the lakes, and the outer highway US 89/191
HeightValley floor around 6,500 ft (2,000 m); peaks rise steeply above it
Known forThe Teton Range, Jenny Lake, moose, short lake hikes, the town of Jackson

How they connect

Yellowstone's South Entrance leads straight into the John D. Rockefeller, Jr. Memorial Parkway and on to Grand Teton. The NPS says the drive from Grand Teton's southern edge to Yellowstone takes a little more than an hour without stops. Each park charges its own entrance fee (page 5).

Plan for driving time. Speed limits are low, roads are busy in summer, and traffic stops for wildlife. A day that looks like 60 miles on the map can take three hours with stops.

Timing

When to go

For a first hiking trip, mid-June to mid-September is the easiest window: roads, lodges and trails are open and the snow is mostly gone from beginner trails.

Late April to early June

Spring

Yellowstone roads reopen in stages from mid-April, and most are open by Memorial Day weekend. Snow lingers on higher trails into June. Baby bison and elk. Some trails close for bear activity.

Mid-June to August

Summer

Everything is open, and it is the busiest time of year. Warm days and cool nights. Afternoon thunderstorms are frequent in July. Start early: the NPS suggests arriving at big sights before 7 am or after 12 pm.

September

Early fall

Fewer people, golden aspens and the elk rut (keep your distance). Nights get cold and early snow is possible at higher elevations. Many facilities still open.

October to November

Late fall

Services close. Dunraven Pass and the Beartooth Highway close in mid-October. Most Yellowstone roads close to cars in early November, and Grand Teton's Teton Park Road closes November 1 to April 30.

Winter (mid-December to mid-March)

Most Yellowstone roads are open only to guided snowcoach and snowmobile tours. The road from the North Entrance to Cooke City via Lamar Valley stays open to cars all year. Beautiful, but not a first-trip season for beginner hikers.

Crowds in summer

Big sights like Old Faithful, Grand Prismatic Spring and Jenny Lake are busiest from about 10 am to 4 pm. Plan hikes for the morning, drive and eat in the middle of the day, and look for wildlife at dawn and dusk.

Plan ahead

Booking timeline

In-park beds and campsites go fast for summer. Book these first, then plan the days around where you sleep.

13 months before

Yellowstone lodges and the five campgrounds run by Yellowstone National Park Lodges (Bridge Bay, Canyon, Fishing Bridge RV Park, Grant Village, Madison). On the 5th of each month, the same month one year later opens. Book at yellowstonenationalparklodges.com.

As early as allowed

Grand Teton lodges (Jackson Lake Lodge, Jenny Lake Lodge, Colter Bay Village, Headwaters Lodge at Flagg Ranch) at gtlc.com, and Signal Mountain Lodge. Check each site for its opening date.

6 months before

The other Yellowstone campgrounds, and every Grand Teton campground, on recreation.gov. The window rolls daily: you can book exactly six months before your date. Grand Teton sites release at 8:00 am Mountain Time.

Any time

Rooms in gateway towns (Gardiner, West Yellowstone, Cody, Jackson), rental cars, and flights. Summer prices rise as dates fill.

On arrival

The Jenny Lake shuttle boat: tickets are sold at the dock (check hours at jennylakeboating.com).

The smart route: fly in one end, out the other

Fly into Bozeman and out of Jackson (or the other way). You drive through both parks once, without backtracking. One-way car rentals can cost more, so compare prices before you book.

Gateway towns

Gardiner, MT (North). Right at the Roosevelt Arch, 15 minutes to Mammoth. Good base for Lamar Valley.

West Yellowstone, MT (West). Biggest choice of rooms, 30 minutes to Madison Junction and the geyser basins.

Cody, WY (East). A real Western town about 50 mi (80 km) outside the East Entrance. Then about an hour more to Lake Village.

Cooke City and Silver Gate, MT (Northeast). Tiny and quiet, closest to Lamar Valley at dawn.

Jackson, WY (South). Restaurants, shops and the main base for Grand Teton.

Inside the parks. The least driving, if you book far ahead (page 5).

Sample plans

3 days and 5 days

3 days: the highlightsFly into Jackson
Day 1
Grand Teton. Shuttle boat across Jenny Lake to Hidden Falls and Inspiration Point (hike 11). Afternoon: String Lake loop or a swim stop. Evening drive north to Yellowstone (sleep at Grant Village, Old Faithful or West Yellowstone).
Day 2
Geyser day. Grand Prismatic Overlook early, before the lot fills. Old Faithful eruption (times are posted at the visitor center), then Observation Point for the view over the basin.
Day 3
Canyon and Hayden Valley. Brink of the Lower Falls, the canyon rim viewpoints, wildlife in Hayden Valley, then south past Yellowstone Lake and back to Jackson. A long day: leave early.
5 days: the full Grand LoopBozeman in, Jackson out
Day 1
North Entrance. Mammoth Hot Springs terraces, then Wraith Falls. Sleep in Gardiner or Mammoth.
Day 2
Lamar Valley at dawn with binoculars (bison, pronghorn, maybe wolves). Trout Lake. Afternoon drive to Canyon.
Day 3
Brink of the Lower Falls and the rim viewpoints. Hayden Valley, then Storm Point by Yellowstone Lake if it is open.
Day 4
Old Faithful and the Upper Geyser Basin, Observation Point. Grand Prismatic Overlook and Fairy Falls in the late afternoon.
Day 5
Drive south into Grand Teton. Jenny Lake boat to Hidden Falls. String Lake loop. Sunset at a Teton viewpoint, sleep in Jackson.

Each day assumes an early start and one main hike. Swap days around if weather or a trail closure gets in the way.

How many days do you need?

Three days shows you the famous sights but means long drives. Five days lets you see every part of the Grand Loop. Seven days adds a summit, a second wildlife dawn and real time in Grand Teton. If you can, give Yellowstone at least four nights and Grand Teton two.

Make it your own

Have more time? Add Phelps Lake at the Laurance S. Rockefeller Preserve, the Beaver Ponds loop at Mammoth, or a second dawn in Lamar Valley. Less energy? Every day here has a short option of under two miles.

Rules of thumb for planning

  • One main hike a day, done before the afternoon heat and storms
  • Sleep near the next day's first stop, not the last one
  • Keep one loose half-day for rain, closures or a great wildlife sighting
  • Fill up with gas and food whenever you pass a village
  • Write your plan on the day pages (24 to 25) and leave a copy with someone at home
Yellowstone & Grand Teton8
Yellowstone in plain words

The geysers and the canyon

West side, Old Faithful to Norris

The geyser basins

Most of the world's active geysers are in Yellowstone, and the biggest groups line the road from Old Faithful north to Norris. The Upper Geyser Basin has Old Faithful and hundreds of other features on boardwalks. Midway Geyser Basin has Grand Prismatic Spring, the largest hot spring in the US. Norris is the hottest and most changeable basin.

Do here: watch Old Faithful (the visitor center posts predicted eruption times), walk the boardwalks, and see Grand Prismatic from above on the Overlook Trail (hike 1).

Center, near Canyon Village

The Grand Canyon of the Yellowstone

A yellow and pink canyon cut by the Yellowstone River, with the Upper Falls and the 308 ft (94 m) Lower Falls. Viewpoints line the North Rim and South Rim drives, and most are a short walk from the car.

Do here: the Brink of the Lower Falls (hike 5) and the rim viewpoints.

Between Canyon and Yellowstone Lake

Hayden Valley

A wide, open valley along the river and one of the best places in the park to see bison, often right next to the road. Watch for elk, waterbirds and sometimes bears at dawn and dusk.

Do here: drive slowly, use the pullouts, stay in or near the car when animals are close, and bring binoculars.

Yellowstone & Grand Teton9
Yellowstone in plain words

The lake, Lamar and Mammoth

Southeast, Fishing Bridge to Grant Village

Yellowstone Lake

A huge high-elevation lake with cold water all summer, beaches, and views of the Absaroka Mountains. West Thumb Geyser Basin sits right on its shore.

Do here: the West Thumb boardwalk, and the Storm Point loop (hike 8) when it is open. The water is very cold: wading only.

Northeast, between Tower and the Northeast Entrance

Lamar Valley

Often called the Serengeti of North America: big herds of bison, plus pronghorn, elk, and the best chance in the park to see wolves, usually from far away through a spotting scope. The road through it is open all year.

Do here: arrive at dawn, park fully off the road, and scan the hillsides. Trout Lake (hike 7) is a short walk nearby.

North, near the North Entrance

Mammoth Hot Springs

Travertine terraces that look like a frozen waterfall, built by hot water over thousands of years. Elk often graze on the lawns of the village, especially in fall. Stay well back from them.

Do here: the Lower and Upper Terraces boardwalks, Wraith Falls (hike 6) and the Beaver Ponds loop (hike 9).

Areas change. Hydrothermal activity can close boardwalks and trails without warning, as it did at Biscuit Basin in July 2024. Check the park's alerts page before each day.

Villages inside Yellowstone

Mammoth, Tower-Roosevelt, Canyon, Lake and Fishing Bridge, Grant Village and Old Faithful have places to eat, stores or lodging, and several have gas stations. Most open from late spring to early fall. Fill up whenever you pass one.

Safety

Thermal areas: stay on the boardwalk

Yellowstone's hot springs are beautiful and deadly. The ground around them can be a thin crust over boiling, acidic water, and people have been badly burned and killed after stepping off the path.

Always

Do

  • Stay on boardwalks and marked trails in every thermal area
  • Hold small children's hands, and keep them on the boardwalk
  • Watch your step on wet, steamy boardwalks
  • Read the signs at each basin: some paths close at short notice
  • Leave the area if you feel sick or dizzy: some thermal areas give off harmful gases
Never

Don't

  • Touch the water or put a hand or foot in a hot spring
  • Swim or soak in thermal pools or their runoff
  • Throw coins, rocks or anything else into a feature
  • Walk on the colorful crust or the white ground for a photo
  • Bring pets: they are not allowed on boardwalks or trails

Why it matters

Water in many hot springs is at or near boiling. The crust can look solid and still break under one person's weight. A fall can cause severe burns in seconds, and help can be far away.

Also good to know

Drones are not allowed in national parks. Geyser eruption times on the NPS app and at the Old Faithful visitor center are predictions, not schedules: come early and be patient. In Grand Teton there are no geyser basins, but the lakes are cold and deep, so wear a life jacket when you paddle.

Yellowstone & Grand Teton19
Safety

Wildlife: give them room

Both parks set the same minimum distances. If an animal changes what it is doing because of you, you are too close: back away.

100 yards
91 m · bears and wolves

About the length of a football field.

25 yards
23 m · all other animals

Bison, elk, moose, deer, pronghorn, coyotes, and everything else.

Bison and elk

Bison look slow but can run faster than you, and the NPS says they have injured more people in Yellowstone than any other animal. Elk are aggressive during the fall rut and in spring when they have calves. Never walk up for a selfie.

On the road

Use pullouts to watch animals, never stop in the lane. Stay in or next to your car when animals are close. Never feed any wildlife, including birds and squirrels.

Bear spray basics

  • Carry one can per person, on your hip or chest strap, not in the pack
  • Learn how the safety clip works before the trail
  • Use it if a bear charges and is close: aim low, in front of the bear
  • Airlines restrict bear spray: buy it near the park and give unused cans away before you fly home

On the trail

Hike in groups of three or more where you can. Make noise, especially near streams and blind corners. Store food in your car or a bear box, never in a tent.

Meet a bear? Stay calm, do not run, and slowly back away. Ask a ranger about recent bear activity on your trail.

Safety

Weather, altitude and sun

You will be hiking at 6,500 to 10,000 ft (2,000 to 3,000 m). The weather changes fast, and summer can bring a warm morning, a hail storm at 2 pm and frost at night.

1

Storms

Afternoon thunderstorms are frequent in July. Start early and plan to be off summits and open ridges by early afternoon.

Hear thunder? Head down, away from peaks and lone trees.
2

Cold

Snow is possible in any month at high elevations, and nights are cold even in summer. Carry a warm layer and a rain jacket on every hike.

July average highs: about 83°F (28°C) at Mammoth, 74°F (23°C) at Yellowstone Lake.
3

Altitude

Thinner air makes climbs feel harder, especially on day one. Go slower than at home, drink water and save the big hike for later in the trip.

Headache, nausea or unusual tiredness? Rest, and go lower if it does not improve.

Sun

There is less air to filter sunlight at this height, and many trails have little shade. Wear a hat and sunglasses, and use sunscreen even on cool days.

Water

Dry air dries you out quickly. Carry at least 1 to 2 liters (34 to 68 fl oz) on a half-day hike, more when it is hot, and treat any water you take from lakes or streams.

Smoke

Wildfire smoke is more likely from mid-summer. Check air quality on airnow.gov and pick shorter, lower hikes on smoky days.

Check every morning

  • Forecast at weather.gov for the area you will hike
  • Park alerts and road status
  • Sunset time, to set your turnaround time

Set a turnaround time

Before you start, pick the time you will turn back even if you have not reached the end: early enough to be down before storms build and back at the car with daylight to spare. Then stick to it. The view will still be there next time.

No signal: what to download and print

Cell service in both parks is patchy at best and usually limited to villages and visitor centers. Do this at home, on Wi-Fi.

Download

  • The free NPS App, then save Yellowstone and Grand Teton for offline use

  • This guide, saved to your phone
  • Your lodging, car and flight confirmations as screenshots or PDFs
  • Your park pass, if you bought a digital one
  • Music, podcasts or audio tours for the long drives

  • The official park maps from nps.gov (also given out at entrances)

In an emergency

Call 911 from any phone that has signal. If you cannot get through, move to higher, open ground, or go to the nearest ranger station, visitor center or lodge. Write the nearest ranger station for each day on your day plan.

Checklist

Packing list for the parks

On every hike (the Ten Essentials)

  • Map, and the NPS App with offline maps
  • Headlamp with fresh batteries
  • Sun protection: hat, sunglasses, sunscreen
  • First aid kit, with blister care
  • Knife or multi-tool
  • Fire starter (use only where fires are allowed)
  • Emergency shelter: a space blanket or bivy
  • Extra food
  • Extra water, 1 to 2 L per person
  • Extra layer: warm top and rain jacket

For these parks

  • Bear spray, one per person, within reach
  • Binoculars or a spotting scope
  • Insect repellent (mosquitoes in early summer)
  • Park pass, ID, and a credit card (no cash at entrances)

Clothes

  • Broken-in hiking shoes or boots
  • Hiking socks, two or more pairs
  • Quick-dry shirts and hiking pants or shorts
  • Fleece or light puffy jacket
  • Waterproof rain jacket
  • Warm hat and light gloves (yes, in July)
  • Sandals for the lodge and lake

In the car

  • Day pack with a hip belt
  • Cooler with lunch and snacks
  • Big refillable water jug
  • Phone charger and a power bank
  • Printed maps and this guide
  • Trash bags: pack out everything
  • Camp chair for wildlife watching

Pack for three seasons in one day

A summer morning near 40°F (4°C) at the trailhead can turn into a hot afternoon and a cold storm. Layers you can add and remove beat one heavy jacket. Keep a dry set of clothes in the car.

Ways to spend less

Picnic lunches from the general stores or a gateway supermarket, one annual pass instead of two park fees, campsites instead of lodges, and shoulder-season dates in early June or September. The best things here are free: geysers, wildlife and the trails.
