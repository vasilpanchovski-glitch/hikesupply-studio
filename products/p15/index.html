Who it is for

First-time visitors and beginner to intermediate hikers. Every hike is a day hike on a marked trail, from a flat lakeshore walk to a half-day climb to a tea house.

How to read the numbers

Distances are round trip, in miles and kilometers. Parks Canada lists most Banff trails one way, so we doubled them. Climb is the total elevation gain Parks Canada gives, in feet and meters. Prices are in Canadian dollars (CAD).

Checked against Parks Canada in November 2026

Shuttle seasons, fees, closures and trail rules change every year, and Jasper is still recovering from the 2024 wildfire. Before you go, check the official pages on page 28 and ask at a visitor centre when you arrive.

The parks

At a glance

Banff is Canada's oldest national park, with the town of Banff and the lakes of Lake Louise. Jasper is the larger, wilder park to the north. The Icefields Parkway links them through a chain of glaciers, lakes and waterfalls.

Where to sleep

Banff town has the most rooms and is the easy base for the first days. Lake Louise village is small and books up early. Jasper town is open and welcoming visitors after the 2024 wildfire. Canmore, just outside Banff's east gate, is often cheaper. A few lodges and hostels sit on the Icefields Parkway itself.

Fly into which city?

Calgary (YYC) for Banff and Lake Louise. Edmonton (YEG) for Jasper. For a Banff-to-Jasper trip, fly into one and out of the other, or loop back on the Parkway.

Timing

When to go: season by season

For a first hiking trip, late June to mid-September gives you thawed lakes, open trails and running shuttles. Late September is the local favorite: golden larches and crisp air, but snow is possible any day.

Jan to MarDeep winter. Skiing, frozen lakes, very short days. The Icefields Parkway needs winter tires or chains and has no services. Many trails are snowshoe or ski only.
April, MaySpring at low elevation, winter higher up. Lakes stay frozen into late May or June. Avalanche risk on many trails. Quiet and good for wildlife watching.
JuneLakes thaw and turn blue. Waterfalls are full. Snow lingers on higher trails. Lake Louise shuttles start mid-May, Moraine Lake shuttles June 1 (2026).
July, AugustPeak season: wildflowers, the most open trails, the most people. Long days. Afternoon thunderstorms are common. Book everything early.
SeptemberCooler, clear days, fewer crowds after Labour Day. Larch trees turn gold, usually mid to late September. Elk rut begins. Snow can fall up high.
OctoberFirst real snows. Shuttles end mid-October (October 12 in 2026). Many trails icy. Quiet and beautiful, but plan for winter driving.
Nov, DecWinter returns. Winter tires or chains required on the Parkway from November 1. Christmas markets and skiing in Banff.

Snow lasts longer than you think

A warm June day in Banff town can still mean deep snow at Lake Agnes or on Parker Ridge. Higher trails can stay snowy into July; Parks Canada says Wilcox Pass can be snowy until late July. Check the trail conditions page before you go.

Passes

Park passes, and crossing from the US

You need a Parks Canada pass to stop anywhere in Banff or Jasper, including the viewpoints on the Icefields Parkway. There is no timed entry: one pass and you can go where you like.

Short trip

Daily admission

$24.50 CAD
Family or group, one vehicle, up to 7 people

Per person: adult (18 to 64) $12.25, senior (65+) $10.75. Youth 17 and under are free. Valid in Banff, Jasper, Yoho and Kootenay. Buy one day for each day you visit.

Week or longer

Discovery Pass

$167.50 CAD
Family or group, 12 months, all Parks Canada places

Adult $83.50, senior $71.50. On a family trip it usually pays off from about the seventh day. Good for every national park in Canada for a year.

2026 prices from parks.canada.ca. Buy online before you go, or at the park gates and visitor centres. In summer 2025 and 2026 Canada offered free admission for part of the season (the Canada Strong Pass): check whether there is an offer for your dates.

Crossing from the US

Documents

US citizens need a passport book to fly in. By land, a passport, passport card, NEXUS card or enhanced driver's license works. Check the rules for every traveler on cbsa-asfc.gc.ca and travel.gc.ca.

Driving

Your US license is fine for a visit. Speeds and distances are in km/h and km, and fuel is sold by the liter. Check that your car insurance or rental covers Canada.

Bear spray

Simplest is to buy it in Banff, Canmore or Jasper. You cannot carry it on a plane, and the border has rules for bringing it by car.

Money

Prices are in CAD. Cards work almost everywhere. Ask your bank about foreign transaction fees before you go.

Canadian visitors

No border to worry about. If you visit several national parks a year, the Discovery Pass is often the better deal.

Banff & Jasper5
Bookings

What to book, and when

The Rockies are busy from June to September. The trick is to book in the right order: rooms or campsites first, then shuttles, then everything else.

Rooms

As soon as you know your dates, especially for July, August and September. Lake Louise village and the Parkway lodges have very few rooms. Free cancellation rates give you room to change the plan.

Campgrounds

On reservation.pc.gc.ca. In 2026 Banff opened on January 23 and Jasper on January 27, at 8 am Mountain Time, for the whole season. Popular dates go in the first hours.

Lake Louise and Moraine Lake shuttle

In 2026 bookings opened April 15 at 8 am Mountain Time with 40% of seats. The other 60% are released at 8 am Mountain Time two days before each day. See page 7.

Roam transit

The Banff to Lake Louise express bus (Route 8X) takes reservations in summer. Book when your dates are fixed.

Park pass

Any time. Buying online saves a stop at the gate.

Tours and boats

Optional extras such as glacier tours and lake cruises book up in summer. Book only once the main plan is fixed.

Planning a 2027 trip?

The dates on this page are from 2026. Parks Canada announces each year's launch dates in the winter before the season. Search "Parks Canada reservations" and "Lake Louise shuttle" in December and January, and set reminders.

Booking sites that are not Parks Canada or Roam may resell shuttle seats at a higher price. For the Parks Canada shuttle, book only on the official Parks Canada reservation site.

Lake Louise and Moraine Lake

Getting there without your car

Moraine Lake Road is closed to personal vehicles all year. Lake Louise has a paid lot that often fills before sunrise. For most visitors the answer is the Parks Canada shuttle.

Easiest

Parks Canada shuttle

$12.75 CAD adult
Senior $6, youth $4, plus $3.50 to book online

From the Lake Louise Park and Ride (free parking for ticket holders), every 30 minutes to both lakes. One ticket includes the Lake Connector bus between the lakes. 2026 season: Lake Louise May 15 to Oct 12, Moraine Lake June 1 to Oct 12. The park pass is extra.

Other ways

Without the shuttle

Bus, bike or tour
Plan ahead for each

Roam Route 8X runs from Banff to the Lake Louise lakeshore all year. Licensed tour companies run trips to Moraine Lake (list on CarFreeBanff.ca). Lake Louise lakeshore parking is $42 a day in 2026 and fills early.

How the shuttle day works

Check in

Arrive at the Park and Ride during your one-hour departure window. Expect a wait of at least 30 minutes, longer at busy times.

Lake hop

The Lake Connector runs every 30 minutes between the two lakes. Boarding is first come, first served. The last bus from Moraine Lake leaves at 6 pm.

Sunrise

The Alpine Start bus leaves the Lake Louise lakeshore at 4 and 5 am for Moraine Lake (2026). You must park at the paid lakeshore lot.

Last bus back

To the Park and Ride at 7:30 pm. Note it on your phone and on page 26.

Cycling

To Lake Louise: 2.5 mi (4 km) one way on Lake Louise Drive. To Moraine Lake: about 8.7 mi (14 km) one way with 1,730 ft (527 m) of climb, for fit riders. Bring a lock.

Walking in?

The Tramline Trail links Lake Louise village to the lakeshore, 2.7 mi (4.3 km) one way. Parks Canada does not recommend walking to Moraine Lake: 17 mi (28 km) round trip on the road.

Scenic drive

The Icefields Parkway

Highway 93 runs about 143 mi (230 km) from Lake Louise to Jasper. Parks Canada says to allow 3 to 4 hours of driving alone. With stops, plan a full day.

Bow LakeAbout 40 min north

A wide lake under the Crowfoot Glacier, right by the road. Great at sunrise.

Peyto Lake viewpointBow Summit

A short, paved uphill walk to the famous wolf-head lake view. Busy at midday.

Mistaya CanyonShort walk

A short downhill walk to a twisting slot canyon carved by the river.

Saskatchewan River CrossingRoughly halfway

Fuel, food and toilets in season. Do not count on them in winter.

Parker RidgeHike 8

A short climb to a ridge with a view of the Saskatchewan Glacier.

Columbia IcefieldHikes 9 and 10

The Athabasca Glacier, the Icefield Centre and the walk to the toe of the glacier.

Sunwapta FallsJasper NP

A powerful waterfall a few steps from the parking area.

Athabasca Falls30 min from Jasper

The river crashes through a narrow gorge. Stay behind the railings.

Before you set out

  • Fill the tank in Lake Louise or Jasper
  • Download offline maps: no cell service on the Parkway
  • Park pass on the dash
  • Water, snacks and warm layers in the car
  • Check the road report at 511.alberta.ca

Winter is different

From November 1 to March 31, snow tires or chains are required. In winter there are no services between Lake Louise and Jasper, and the road can close for avalanche control. Drive it in daylight and only in good weather.

Safety

Wildlife: bears, elk and more

Grizzly and black bears, elk, bighorn sheep, mountain goats, moose and wolves all live here. Seeing them is a highlight. Getting close is how people and animals get hurt.

100 m
330 ft · about 10 bus lengths

Bears, wolves, cougars and coyotes.

30 m
100 ft · about 3 bus lengths

Elk, deer, bighorn sheep and moose.

Parks Canada's minimum distances. If an animal moves because of you, you are too close. Feeding or disturbing wildlife is illegal, with fines up to $25,000.

On the trail

  • Carry bear spray where you can reach it, and know how to use it
  • Make noise, especially near streams and blind corners
  • Hike in a group and keep children close
  • Keep dogs on a leash at all times
  • Read the trail notices at the trailhead

If you meet a bear

  • Stay calm and do not run
  • Speak in a firm voice and back away slowly
  • Give it a way out
  • Have your bear spray ready
  • Report it to the park

Group rules near Moraine Lake

Because of bears, Parks Canada often requires hikers to travel in a tight group of four or more on trails near Moraine Lake, usually from mid-July to mid-October. This includes Larch Valley, Sentinel Pass, Consolation Lakes, Eiffel Lake and Paradise Valley. Not the lakeshore or the Rockpile. Check the bulletins for your dates.

On the road

If you see wildlife, slow down and stay in your vehicle. Stop only where it is safe, with hazard lights on, take a quick look and move on. Never get out for a photo.

Banff & Jasper9
Safety

Mountain weather

In the Rockies you can get sun, rain, hail and snow on the same summer hike. It is colder up high, wind makes it feel colder still, and storms build fast in the afternoon.

1

Dress in layers

A wicking base layer, a warm fleece or puffy, and a waterproof jacket with a hood, on every hike.

Pack a hat and light gloves, even in July.
2

Start early

Summer storms often build after midday. Mornings are calmer, cooler and less crowded.

Be off open ridges by early afternoon.
3

Turn back in time

Pick a turnaround time before you start and keep it, even if the top looks close.

The mountain will still be there.

Thunderstorms

  • Watch for clouds growing tall by late morning
  • Thunder means you are in range: head down
  • Get off ridges and away from lone trees
  • Spread a group out
  • A hard-top car is good shelter

Cold and wet

  • Hypothermia can happen in summer when wet and tired
  • Change out of wet layers when you stop
  • Snow can fall any month on high trails
  • Carry a space blanket and a headlamp
  • Check the forecast every morning

Glacier water

The lakes and rivers are fed by snow and ice. They are cold enough to cause cold shock in seconds, and the currents are strong. Stay back from riverbanks and waterfall edges, and do not drink untreated water.

Sample plans

3 days: Banff and Lake Louise

3 days, one baseSleep in Banff or Canmore
Day 1
Arrive from Calgary. Short walk at the Fenland Trail or up Tunnel Mountain (hike 2) to stretch your legs. Evening drive to Lake Minnewanka and back, watching for wildlife.
Day 2
Early shuttle to Lake Louise. Walk the shoreline, then climb to the Lake Agnes Tea House (hike 4). Lake Connector to Moraine Lake in the afternoon for the shoreline and the Rockpile view. Last bus back.
Day 3
Johnston Canyon at opening time if it is open (hike 1), or Bow Lake and the Peyto Lake viewpoint on the Icefields Parkway (page 8). Back to Calgary in the afternoon.

Rules of thumb

  • One big outing a day, started early
  • Book the shuttle as soon as reservations open
  • Keep the last day light: you still have a drive
  • Always have a rainy-day backup (page 23)

Only have one day?

Spend it at Lake Louise and Moraine Lake on the shuttle. Walk to the Fairview Lookout or the Lake Agnes Tea House, then ride the Lake Connector to Moraine Lake.

Where to sleep

Banff or Canmore

One base for all three nights means no packing up. Banff town is closest to the Roam buses; Canmore is about 25 minutes east and often cheaper.

Getting around

Car plus shuttle

A rental car from Calgary for days 1 and 3. On day 2, drive to the Lake Louise Park and Ride, or take Roam Route 8X from Banff.

Moderate

WhereMoraine Lake, by the shuttle. The trail starts near the lodge.

Hike 9

Wilcox Pass

Moderate
Round trip5 mi8 km
Climb1,280 ft390 m
Time2 to 3 hTo the pass
BestLate July to SeptSnow into July

WhereIcefields Parkway, by the Wilcox Creek Campground road, 3.1 km (1.9 mi) south of the Icefield Centre.

What you seeOne of the best views in the Rockies for the effort: a climb out of the trees to open meadows facing the Athabasca Glacier and the peaks of the Columbia Icefield. Bighorn sheep often graze up here.

Hike 10

Toe of the Athabasca Glacier

Easy
Round trip1.1 mi1.8 km
Climb200 ft60 m
TimeAbout 1 hParks Canada
BestJune to SeptCold wind

WhereAcross from the Icefield Centre, at the end of the Toe of the Glacier road.

What you seeA short walk over bare rock left by the retreating glacier, with markers showing where the ice reached in past years, to a viewpoint near the glacier's edge.

Good to knowDo not walk onto the glacier. Parks Canada warns that the ice at the toe is hollow and collapsing, and hidden crevasses have killed people. Bring a jacket, gloves and a warm hat.

Next door: the Forefield Trail, 2.2 mi (3.6 km) round trip with almost no climb, crosses the open ground in front of the glacier. On warm afternoons the meltwater streams can rise: be ready to turn back.

Plan B

Rainy-day and rest-day ideas

Rain, smoke or tired legs are part of every mountain trip. Keep one of these in your pocket.

Banff Upper Hot Springs

Banff town

A warm pool with a mountain view, run by Parks Canada. Good on a cold or wet day. Roam Route 1 goes there.

Cave and Basin

Banff town

The national historic site where Canada's national parks began, with exhibits and a short boardwalk.

Visitor centres

Banff, Lake Louise, Jasper

Maps, exhibits, current trail reports and rangers to ask. Lake Louise has good displays on the geology of the Rockies.

Museums in town

Banff and Jasper

Small museums on the history and art of the Rockies. Check opening hours before you go.

Lake cruises and gondolas

When the cloud lifts

Lake Minnewanka and Maligne Lake boat tours, and gondolas in Banff and Jasper. All need tickets: check the weather first.

Short, low walks

Under 2 mi

Fenland Trail and the Bow River in Banff, the Lake Louise shoreline, or Athabasca Falls. Easy to cut short.

Thunder nearby? Leave ridges and open shores, get into a hard-top car or a building and wait about 30 minutes after the last thunder.

A rest day is not a wasted day

On a 5 or 7-day trip, one slow day keeps everyone happy for the big hikes. Do laundry, buy picnic food, and plan tomorrow with the weather forecast.

Checklist

Packing list for the Rockies

For every hike (the Ten Essentials)

  • Map, plus offline maps on your phone
  • Headlamp with fresh batteries
  • Sun protection: hat, sunglasses, sunscreen
  • First aid kit, with blister care
  • Knife or multi-tool
  • Fire starter (emergencies only)
  • Emergency shelter: a space blanket
  • Extra food
  • Extra water, plus a filter or tablets
  • Extra layer: warm top and rain jacket

For these parks

  • Bear spray (buy it locally)
  • Park pass and passport
  • Shuttle tickets (screenshot them)
  • Traction devices outside midsummer

Layers for mountain weather

  • Wicking base layer (no cotton)
  • Fleece or light puffy jacket
  • Waterproof rain jacket with a hood
  • Hiking pants, plus shorts for warm days
  • Warm hat and light gloves, even in summer
  • Broken-in hiking shoes with good grip
  • Hiking socks, plus a dry spare pair

For the car

  • Full fuel tank before the Parkway
  • More water and snacks
  • Phone charger and power bank
  • This guide, printed or offline
  • Small trash bag: pack out everything
  • Dry clothes and shoes for after

Dress like an onion

A frosty start at the lake, a warm climb and a windy viewpoint are all normal on the same hike. Start a little cold, take a layer off before you sweat, and put one back on when you stop.

Ways to spend less

Youth 17 and under get in free. One family pass covers up to seven people in one car. Rooms in Canmore are often cheaper than in Banff. Picnic lunches from a grocery store in town cost far less than restaurant meals at the lakes.

Before every hike

Safety, Leave No Trace and official sources

Safety

Every hike

  • Tell someone your plan and when you will be back
  • Set a turnaround time and keep it
  • Check trail conditions, closures and warnings
  • Emergency: call 911. Signal is patchy, so do not rely on it
  • Go back if the group or the weather says so
Leave No Trace

Every visit

  • Stay on the trail, even when muddy
  • Pack out all trash, peels and tissues
  • Never feed any animal
  • Leave rocks, flowers and antlers where they are
  • No drones in national parks without a permit
