How to use this guide

Glacier is big, wild and steep, with one famous road across the middle and grizzly bears on the trails. Most of the planning comes down to three questions: is the road open, how will you reach the trailhead, and where will you sleep.

Who it is for

First-time visitors and beginner to intermediate hikers. Every hike here is a day hike on a marked trail, from a flat boardwalk loop to a full day on the Highline.

How to read the hikes

The park lists trail distances one way. We doubled them, so the distances here are round trip, in miles and kilometers. Climbs are the elevation gain the National Park Service gives, in feet and meters.

Checked against nps.gov in November 2026

Glacier changed its access rules for 2026, and it may change them again for 2027. Road openings, shuttles, fees and closures change every year, and snow or bears can close trails at short notice. Before you go, check the official pages on page 28 and ask a ranger when you arrive.

Print it or keep it offline

Cell service is poor or missing in most of the park. Save this PDF to your phone before you leave home, or print the pages you need. Pages 25 to 27 and 29 are made for printing.

Three things that surprise first-timers

There is no gas station anywhere inside the park. The high part of Going-to-the-Sun Road is only open for a few months each summer (page 4). And each area of the east side is its own dead-end valley, so "nearby" on the map can mean an hour in the car.

Timing

When to go: month by month

For a first hiking trip, mid-July to mid-September gives you the whole road and the most snow-free trails. No one can predict the exact opening date of the high road: it depends on the winter's snow.

Jan to MarWinter. The lower parts of Going-to-the-Sun Road stay open, so you can reach Lake McDonald. Snowshoeing and skiing, very few services.
April, MayCrews plow Going-to-the-Sun Road from early April. Low trails are muddy, high trails deep in snow. Snowstorms and avalanches are still common in May.
JuneThe alpine section usually opens between mid-June and early July (in 2026 the whole road was open on June 22). Waterfalls are full, snow still covers high trails.
JulyThe busiest month. Wildflowers start, snow melts off the high trails, and in 2026 the Logan Pass shuttle and 3-hour limit started on July 1.
AugustWarm days, the most trails open, busy trailheads. Wildfire smoke is possible: check air quality before a big hike.
SeptemberQuieter after Labor Day. In 2026 the shuttle and parking limit ended September 7. Cold mornings, first snow up high possible.
OctoberThe whole road is usually open until the third Monday of October, weather allowing. Gold larches on the west side, many services closed.
Nov, DecWinter returns. Lower roads only. Winter entrance rates apply from November 1 to April 30 (page 5).

Booking a trip for late June?

Keep it flexible. If the alpine section is not open yet, you can still enjoy Lake McDonald, Avalanche Lake, Many Glacier, Two Medicine and the St. Mary side by driving around on US 2. The plans on pages 12 to 15 include swaps.

Entry

Entry, passes and what changed in 2026

For 2026, Glacier dropped the vehicle reservations it used in earlier summers. You only need a park pass to drive in. The catch: when an area gets too full, cars can be turned away for a while.

In 2026

No vehicle reservations anywhere: not for Going-to-the-Sun Road, Many Glacier, Two Medicine or the North Fork. When an area reaches capacity, rangers may temporarily divert cars. People with a booking in that area (lodging, camping, boat tour, horseback ride, guided hike) or a backcountry permit can still get in, though there may be a wait.

Logan Pass

Logan Pass: shuttle ticket or 3 hours

Logan Pass, at 6,646 ft (2,026 m), is the top of Going-to-the-Sun Road and the start of the Hidden Lake and Highline trails. In summer 2026 there were two ways to get there.

Option 1

Drive and park

3 hours
July 1 to September 7, 2026

Take a free timestamped permit from the kiosk and put it on your dashboard. The limit applied 24 hours a day. Enough for the visitor center and Hidden Lake Overlook. The lot usually fills by sunrise.

Option 2

Shuttle ticket

All day
Needed for the Highline

A ticketed shuttle from the west or east side. Required for longer hikes from Logan Pass, such as the Highline to Granite Park Chalet or to The Loop. $1 processing fee per ticket.

Booking

Recreation.gov or 877-444-6777, not in the park. Everyone 2 and older needs a ticket, for one day, one boarding place and one time. Photo ID may be asked for.

Release

Some tickets 60 days ahead (8 am Mountain Time, rolling, from May 2), the rest at 7 pm Mountain Time the evening before (from June 30).

Coming back

Shuttles left Logan Pass about every 60 to 90 minutes. Last bus: 7:30 pm west, 8 pm east. Miss it and there is no other ride.

Not on the shuttle in 2026: Avalanche Lake and Trail of the Cedars. Drive there early. Pets are not allowed on the shuttle, and bear spray must be secured.
Glacier National Park6
Planning

What to book, and when

Rooms near Glacier are few for the number of visitors, and the summer season is short. Book the bed first, then build the days around it.

Lodging in the park

Historic lodges and motor inns at Lake McDonald, Apgar, Many Glacier, Rising Sun and Swiftcurrent, run by park concessioners. Most open only in summer and book up many months ahead. Two hike-in chalets, Granite Park and Sperry, need a reservation and a long walk.

Gateway towns

West side: West Glacier, Columbia Falls, Whitefish and Kalispell. East side: St. Mary, Babb and East Glacier Park. Split your nights, west then east, to save hours of driving.

Campgrounds

Several park campgrounds take reservations on Recreation.gov, and some are first-come, first-served. Check the camping pages on nps.gov/glac for which, when they open, and how far ahead you can book.

Boat tours

Concession boats run on Lake McDonald, St. Mary Lake (from Rising Sun), Swiftcurrent Lake and Lake Josephine (Many Glacier) and Two Medicine Lake. Fees apply. Some boats shorten hikes a lot (pages 18 to 21). The park says reservations are highly recommended.

Logan Pass shuttle

Recreation.gov, 60 days ahead or the night before (page 6). Set a phone alarm.

Getting there

Glacier Park International Airport near Kalispell is about 30 mi (48 km) from the West Entrance. Amtrak's Empire Builder stops at West Glacier all year and at East Glacier Park in season.

Rule of thumb

Lodging or campsite first, rental car second, boat tours third, shuttle tickets last. A booking in Many Glacier or Two Medicine also lets you in when that area is full (page 8).

The east valleys

Many Glacier, Two Medicine and the North Fork

These quieter corners hold some of the best hikes in the park. Each one is reached by a single road, and each can fill up. Arrive early and keep a Plan B.

Many Glacier

20 mi from St. Mary

Lakes ringed by cliffs, moose, bighorn sheep and grizzlies on the slopes. Grinnell Lake, Redrock Falls, Iceberg Lake and Apikuni Falls start here (pages 19 and 20).

Two Medicine

Southeast corner

A calm lake under tall peaks, with short waterfall walks and a boat. In 2026 the North Shore Trailhead was closed by campground construction (page 21).

North Fork

Northwest corner

Remote and rustic. Long gravel roads, the small community of Polebridge, Bowman and Kintla lakes. No services in the park there: come with a full tank and food.

St. Mary

East end of the road

The east gate of Going-to-the-Sun Road, with a visitor center, lodging and the waterfall hikes on page 18.

If you are turned away

  • Ask the ranger roughly how long the wait may be
  • Drive to the next area on your list and come back later
  • Many Glacier full? Try Two Medicine, or St. Mary and the falls on page 18
  • Use the time for a scenic drive or a visitor center
  • Never park along the road outside marked spots

Glacier National Park8
Safety

Bears and wildlife

Glacier is home to both grizzly bears and black bears, plus moose, mountain goats and bighorn sheep. Seeing them is a highlight. A few simple habits keep you and the animals safe.

100 yards
91 m from bears and wolves

Approaching closer on purpose is against park rules.

25 yards
23 m from all other wildlife

Goats, sheep, deer, elk and moose included.

On the trail

Every hike

  • Carry bear spray where you can grab it fast, and know how to use it
  • Talk, call out or clap often, so bears hear you coming
  • Hike in a group: the park reports no attacks on groups of four or more
  • Do not run on trails
  • Extra care near streams, thick brush, berry patches and at dawn and dusk
If you meet a bear

Stay calm

  • Moving toward you on the trail? Step aside and let it pass
  • Calm bear: back away slowly, talking quietly
  • Never run. Get your spray ready
  • Defensive charge and no spray? Lie on your stomach, hands behind your neck, pack on
  • If an attack goes on, fight back

Food and scented items

Never leave food, trash or scented things alone, even in your pack for a minute. At camp, keep them in a car or a food locker, day and night.

Good to know

Bear bells are not considered effective: use your voice. Size and color do not tell you the species. Report every bear you see to a ranger. Taking bear spray into Canada? Only EPA-approved canisters are allowed.

Summary of the park's bear safety page. Read the full page on nps.gov/glac and watch the park's bear spray video before your trip.

Glacier National Park9
Safety

Weather, snow and cold water

Glacier's weather changes fast, and the high country keeps its snow long into summer. The park also warns that its lakes and streams stay cold all year.

1

Snow into July

High trails can stay snowy well after the road opens. Popular routes like the Highline may stay closed for hazardous snow.

Check the park's trail status page the night before.
2

Steep snowfields

Snow can hide holes and running water underneath. Do not cross steep snow without the skills and gear for it: turn around.

Never walk on snow above a drop or a lake.
3

Fast weather

Mountain weather can be very different from the valley towns. Start early, and turn back if it gets cold fast or storms build.

Get off open ridges when thunder starts.

Cold, fast water

  • Stay back from streams and waterfalls: rocks by the water are slippery
  • Do not wade or swim in swift water
  • Wear a life jacket in boats and kayaks
  • Cold water can take your breath away in seconds
  • Watch children closely near water and edges

Stay warm and dry

  • Rain jacket and a warm layer on every hike
  • No cotton: it stays wet and cold
  • Hypothermia can happen above freezing
  • Hat and gloves, even in July, on the east side
  • Treat stream water: boil it 1 minute or use a filter

Stay on the trail

Falls are a real risk off the trail and past fences and railings, often while someone is getting a photo. Stay on marked paths, stay behind barriers, and keep children close at overlooks and on the Highline ledge.

Vehicle limits

Vehicles over 21 ft (6.4 m) long or 8 ft (2.4 m) wide, including bumpers and mirrors, may not drive between Avalanche Creek and Rising Sun. Over 10 ft (3 m) tall may have trouble west of Logan Pass. Speed limit 25 mph in the alpine section.

Good to know

No gas in the park: fill up before. An EV charger is across from the Lake McDonald Lodge camp store. Bikes are banned on parts of the road on summer afternoons: check the dates. Text GNPROADS to 333111 for road updates.

Arrive at West Glacier. Apgar, Lake McDonald, and Trail of the Cedars in the evening light (hike 1).

Make it your own

Strong hikers can swap Hidden Lake for the Highline with a shuttle ticket (hike 4). Traveling with small children? Swap Grinnell Lake for the Swiftcurrent Nature Trail and Running Eagle Falls (hike 11).

Booking order for this plan

  • Rooms for 2 nights west and 1 night east, as early as you can
  • Rental car, and fill the tank before you enter
  • Many Glacier boat tickets if you want the Grinnell Lake shortcut
  • Logan Pass shuttle tickets if you plan the Highline
  • Park pass: one 7-day vehicle pass covers all 3 days

Pace yourself

Glacier days are long in summer, with light until late evening. That is a gift, not a goal: keep one afternoon free and do not try to see every area in one trip.

Why a shuttle ticket on day 3

In 2026 a car at Logan Pass could only stay 3 hours, which is too short for the Highline. With a ticket you can leave the car at Apgar and spend the whole day up high. Check the shuttle times for your year and the last bus back.

Keep in your pocket

  • Shuttle ticket screenshot
  • Turnaround time written down
  • Bear spray on your hip belt
  • Extra layer and rain jacket
  • Snacks and 2 liters of water

Rainy day swap

Low cloud at Logan Pass hides everything. Swap day 3 with day 2 and walk in the forest, or see page 23 for indoor ideas.

Set your Highline turnaround

Before you start, work out when you must be back at Logan Pass for your shuttle, and turn around at the halfway point of the time you have. The way back is just as long, and the last bus does not wait.

A day in Waterton Lakes (Canada)

Glacier and Canada's Waterton Lakes National Park together form the Waterton-Glacier International Peace Park. Waterton has its own Parks Canada pass. Every crossing needs a passport. The Chief Mountain crossing is seasonal (May 15 to September 30, hours vary); the Piegan/Carway crossing north of Babb is on US 89. Check border hours and what you may carry before you go.

Checklist

Packing list for Glacier

For every hike (the Ten Essentials)

  • Map, plus the NPS App with offline maps
  • Headlamp with fresh batteries
  • Sun protection: hat, sunglasses, sunscreen
  • First aid kit, with blister care
  • Knife or multi-tool
  • Fire starter (for emergencies only)
  • Emergency shelter: a space blanket
  • Extra food
  • Extra water, plus a filter or tablets
  • Extra layer: warm top and rain jacket

For this park

  • Bear spray in a holster, one per adult
  • Shuttle ticket and park pass (screenshots)
  • A card: the park is cashless
  • Binoculars for goats, sheep and bears
  • Passport if you may visit Waterton

Layers for mountain weather

  • Moisture-wicking base layer (no cotton)
  • Fleece or light puffy jacket
  • Waterproof rain jacket with a hood
  • Hiking pants or zip-offs
  • Warm hat and light gloves, even in summer
  • Broken-in hiking shoes with good grip
  • Hiking socks, plus a dry spare pair

For the car

  • A full tank: no gas inside the park
  • More water and snacks for the drive
  • Phone charger and power bank
  • Printed maps and this guide
  • Small trash bag: pack out everything
  • Dry clothes and shoes for after

Flying in? Buy bear spray there

Bear spray is not allowed on airplanes, in carry-on or checked bags. Buy or rent it in a gateway town or at a park store, and practice taking it out of the holster before your first hike.

Before every hike

Safety, Leave No Trace and official sources

Safety

Every hike

  • Tell someone your plan and when you will be back
  • Set a turnaround time and keep it
  • Check trail status, closures and bear warnings
  • Emergency: call 911. Signal is patchy, so do not rely on it
  • Go back if the group or the weather says so
Leave No Trace

Every visit

  • Stay on the trail, even when muddy or snowy
  • Pack out all trash, peels and tissues
  • Never feed any animal, even a ground squirrel
  • Leave rocks, flowers and antlers where they are
  • No drones: they are not allowed in national parks
