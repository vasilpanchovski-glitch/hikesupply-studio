How to use this guide

Acadia is small for a national park, but it packs in a lot: granite mountains that drop to the sea, quiet ponds, sand and cobble beaches, and 45 miles of old carriage roads. It is also one of the busiest parks in the country, on an island with narrow roads. Good planning is mostly about timing and parking.

Acadia National Park2
The park

Acadia at a glance

The park is a patchwork of land mixed with private towns and villages. You drive in and out of the park many times a day. The 27-mile Park Loop Road ties the main sights together.

Mount Desert IslandMost of the park

The main event: Cadillac Mountain, the Park Loop Road, Sand Beach, Jordan Pond, the carriage roads and most of the hikes. Bar Harbor is the big town. The island is linked to the mainland by a bridge at Trenton.

The quiet sideWest of Somes Sound

Southwest Harbor, Bass Harbor Head Lighthouse, Echo Lake, Ship Harbor and Acadia Mountain. Fewer people, same granite and sea.

Schoodic PeninsulaAbout 1 hour's drive east

The park's only part on the mainland. A one-way loop road past wave-washed ledges at Schoodic Point, with a campground and bike paths. Much quieter than the island.

Isle au HautBy mailboat from Stonington

A remote island with rugged coastal trails and the small Duck Harbor campground. A full-day trip, for people who want solitude.

Three things that surprise first-timers

There are no hotels inside the park: you sleep in a campground or in a nearby town. Parking at the famous stops fills by mid-morning in summer. And the summit road up Cadillac Mountain needs its own timed reservation for most of the year (page 6).

Timing

When to go: season by season

Summer and fall color bring the crowds. Late May, June and September are the sweet spots for many hikers: everything is open and the lots are a little calmer.

Dec to MarchMost of the Park Loop Road is closed to cars (from December 1). Snow and ice on trails, quiet carriage roads for skiing and snowshoeing. Many town businesses close. Campgrounds are closed: no winter camping.
AprilThe Park Loop Road opens April 15. Mud season: carriage roads close for the thaw. Cold, wet and quiet. Some peregrine falcon cliffs and trails closed.
May, JuneThe park wakes up. Campgrounds and visitor centers open in May. Cool days, black flies and mosquitoes, fog some mornings. The Island Explorer starts (May 20 in 2026).
July, AugustPeak season: warm days, all bus routes running, very busy parking lots. Sea water is still cold, about 55°F (13°C) at Sand Beach.
SeptemberCalmer after Labor Day, warm days, cool nights. Still busy on weekends and cruise ship days in Bar Harbor.
OctoberFall color, usually best in early to mid-October, and big crowds to see it. Cold mornings on the summits. Many services wind down after Columbus Day / Indigenous Peoples' Day.
NovemberQuiet, cold, short days, hunting season outside the park. The Park Loop Road closes for the winter on December 1.

Where to buy

Online (go.nps.gov/AcadiaPass or Recreation.gov), or at fee machines and visitor centers in the park. Since April 15, 2026 the park is cashless: card or mobile pay only.

Visiting from Canada?

You count as a non-resident, so the $100 fee per person 16+ applies, unless you buy the $250 non-resident annual pass. Do the sums for your group on page 27.

Free entrance days

A few days a year are free for US citizens and residents. The Cadillac reservation fee still applies on those days. Check the list for your year on nps.gov/acad/planyourvisit/fees.htm.

Acadia National Park6
Getting around

The Island Explorer, parking and bikes

Parking is the hardest part of a summer day in Acadia. The best trick in the park is free: leave the car and ride the Island Explorer.

Island Explorer

Fare-free propane buses to Bar Harbor, the Park Loop Road, Jordan Pond, Sand Beach, campgrounds and villages. You still need a park pass. In 2026 some routes started May 20 and the full service June 23, running into mid-October.

Where to park for it

Free all-day parking at the Acadia Gateway Center in Trenton, before the bridge to the island. Buses into the park from there.

Bikes on the bus

Racks for six bikes on each bus. No e-bikes, tandems or fat tires on the racks. Leashed or crated pets may ride, but not on seats.

Not on the bus

Cadillac Summit Road, the Precipice trailhead and Ship Harbor have no bus service. Plan a car, or a bike, for those.

Summer parking

Sand Beach, Jordan Pond and the Bubbles fill early, often by 9 or 10 am. Arrive before 8 am, come after 4 pm, or take the bus. Never park on the grass or the roadside where signs say no: it blocks traffic and gets ticketed or towed.

Driving tips

  • Part of the Park Loop Road is one-way: you can't turn back
  • Parking in the right lane of the one-way part is allowed only where signed
  • Fill the tank in Bar Harbor or Ellsworth
  • Watch for cyclists and walkers on the road

Bikes

Bikes go on the Park Loop Road and the carriage roads, never on hiking trails. Only Class 1 e-bikes (pedal assist up to 20 mph) are allowed on carriage roads. Rent in Bar Harbor; Maine law requires helmets under 16.

Bus routes and dates change every year: check exploreacadia.com before you plan around it.

Planning

Where to stay

There is no lodge or hotel inside Acadia. Pick a campground or a town, and book early for July, August and October.

Blackwoods

Park campground on the east side of the island, close to Cadillac, Sand Beach and the Ocean Path. Open May 1 to October 19 in 2026.

Seawall

Park campground on the quiet side, near Ship Harbor and Bass Harbor. Open May 20 to October 12 in 2026.

Schoodic Woods

Park campground on the Schoodic Peninsula, the quietest of the three. Open May 20 to October 12 in 2026.

Bar Harbor

The busy hub: inns, hotels, food, bus routes, the Bar Island land bridge. Most choice, highest summer prices.

The quiet side

Southwest Harbor, Northeast Harbor, Bass Harbor: smaller villages, calmer evenings, a longer drive to Sand Beach.

Off the island

Trenton and Ellsworth: often cheaper, near the Gateway Center bus park-and-ride. Winter Harbor for Schoodic.

Sample plans

1 day: the Park Loop Road highlights

A full day on the east sideLate May to October
Before 8
Start at Sand Beach before the lot fills. Walk the Ocean Path (hike 1) to Thunder Hole and Otter Cliffs and back.
Late morning
Drive or bus to Jordan Pond. Walk the easy east side of the Jordan Pond Path (hike 2), or the Bubbles (hike 5) for a view over the pond.
Lunch
The Jordan Pond House (seasonal) for its popovers, or a picnic. Keep food packed up and never feed wildlife.
Afternoon
Your daytime Cadillac slot: drive the summit road and walk the short summit loop. Layers: it is windy up there.
Evening
Dinner in Bar Harbor. At low tide, walk out on the Bar Island land bridge (hike 3), but watch the time.

No Cadillac reservation?

Hike up instead (Cadillac North Ridge, hike 8), or swap the afternoon for Sieur de Monts and the Wild Gardens of Acadia, near the start of the Park Loop Road.

Rules of thumb

  • One main hike, started early
  • Park once, then use the bus and your feet
  • Check the tide table and the Cadillac time the night before
  • Fill the tank and pack lunch before you enter

Leave time to look

Some of Acadia's best moments are sitting on a warm granite ledge, watching the sea. Plan less than you think you can do.

Schoodic Point and the waves

Storm swells from far out at sea can throw big waves over the ledges even on a sunny day. Stay well back from wet, dark rock and never turn your back on the ocean.

Rainy-day and low-tide plans

Rainy or foggy day

What to do

  • Skip the summits and all rung and ladder trails: wet granite is very slippery
  • Walk a carriage road loop, wide gravel and no ledges (page 20)
  • The Jordan Pond east side, or Ship Harbor in the mist
  • Sieur de Monts, the Nature Center and the Wild Gardens (seasonal)
  • Abbe Museum in Bar Harbor, on the Wabanaki people of Maine
  • Sand Beach and Otter Cliffs from the overlooks in fog
Low tide

Plan around it

  • The tide here rises and falls about 10 ft (3 m) or more, twice a day
  • Bar Island land bridge: from 1.5 hours before to 1.5 hours after low tide
  • Tide pools at Ship Harbor and Wonderland: go on a falling tide
  • Thunder Hole booms best 1 to 2 hours before high tide
  • Tide pools: look, don't take, and step only on bare rock
  • Write the times on your day planner (page 25)

Acadia National Park12
Big moment

Sunrise on Cadillac Mountain

At 1,530 ft (466 m), Cadillac is the highest point on the North Atlantic coast of the US, and one of the first places in the country to see the sun rise for part of the year. It is also very popular.

Book it

Sunrise slot, $6

In season you need a sunrise vehicle reservation (one per vehicle every 7 days). 30 percent open 90 days ahead, the rest 2 days ahead, at 10 am Eastern.

Or walk up

No reservation on foot

Fit hikers can climb the North Ridge (hike 8) in the dark with headlamps. Allow plenty of time and know the way down.

It is cold

Even in July

Wind on the summit makes it feel much colder than town. Bring a warm jacket, hat, gloves, a blanket and a hot drink.

Clouds happen

Have a plan B

Fog can hide the sunrise. Check the forecast; the summit walk is still lovely after the cloud lifts.

Sunrise checklist

  • Reservation QR code saved offline
  • Park pass on the windshield
  • Alarm 90 minutes before sunrise, plus drive time
  • Headlamp for the dark parking lot
  • Warm layers, hat, gloves, blanket
  • Breakfast and a thermos

On the summit

Stay on the paved paths and bare rock: the thin plants on the summit take years to grow back. Keep your voice low for the people around you.

Hike 1

Ocean Path

Easy
One way2.2 mi3.5 km
ClimbGentleA few steps
Time1 to 2 hoursOne way, NPS
BestEarly or lateFewer people

WhereSand Beach, upper parking lot (walk up the steps behind the restrooms). Island Explorer stops at Sand Beach, Thunder Hole and Otter Point.

What you seeThe classic Acadia walk along pink granite cliffs: Sand Beach, Thunder Hole, Monument Cove and the 110 ft (34 m) Otter Cliffs. Take the bus back, or walk out and back.

Good to knowThe first 0.7 mi (1.1 km) is wide and gentle; past Thunder Hole it gets narrower and rougher. Stay on the path and away from wet, dark rock at the water's edge.

Thunder Hole is loudest 1 to 2 hours before high tide. In big swells, stay off the lower rocks: waves have swept people away.

Jordan Pond Path

Time1 to 3 hoursNPS

Acadia National Park19
Walk and bike

The carriage roads

About 45 miles (72 km) of broken-stone roads, built in the early 1900s with granite bridges and no cars allowed. Wide, smooth and gentle: perfect for walking, biking, strollers and skiing in winter.

Who goes where

Shared roads

Walkers, bikes and horses share the carriage roads. Bikes, e-bikes and horses are never allowed on hiking trails.

E-bikes

Class 1 only

Pedal-assist e-bikes up to 20 mph are allowed. Class 2 and 3 (throttle or faster) are not.

Mud season

Closed in the thaw

The roads close in spring and sometimes after heavy rain to protect the surface. Check alerts.

Private sections

No bikes

Some carriage roads outside the park are private. Bikes are not allowed there: follow the signs.

Etiquette

Who yields

  • Cyclists yield to everyone
  • Walkers yield to horses
  • Keep right, pass on the left with a friendly call
  • Speed limit 20 mph (32 km/h) for everyone
  • Step aside when you stop
Good loops

Easy ideas

  • Around Eagle Lake: flat and scenic
  • Jordan Pond to the Bubble Pond bridge
  • Around Witch Hole Pond, near Hulls Cove
  • Ask at a visitor center for a carriage road map

Signposts

Every junction has a numbered wooden signpost. Carry the park's carriage road map and write your route by numbers, for example 6, 7, 8, 9. It makes getting lost hard.

Acadia National Park20
Safety

Trail safety on granite and by the sea

Acadia's trails are short but rough. Most injuries come from slips on wet rock, falls on steep steps going down, and getting caught by the sea.

1

Wet rock

Granite is slick after rain, in fog and near water. Wait for it to dry before rung trails or ledges.

Good grip shoes, not sandals.
2

Cliffs

Many edges have no railings. Stay well back, especially for photos, and keep children within reach.

If you need to lean, it is too close.
3

The ocean

Big waves can surge over low rocks at Thunder Hole, Otter Cliffs and Schoodic Point, even on calm, sunny days.

Never turn your back on the sea.

Finding the way

  • Follow the blue paint blazes on trees and rock
  • On open rock, follow the cairns (rock piles)
  • Never move cairns or build new ones
  • Carry a real trail map: phone signal is patchy
  • Fog can hide the next cairn: turn back if you lose the trail

Weather and time

  • Fog can roll in fast from the sea
  • Thunderstorm? Get off summits and ledges
  • Carry at least 1 liter (32 oz) of water each, more on warm days
  • Bring a headlamp: short hikes run late
  • Pick a turnaround time and keep it
Emergency: call 911. Know your trail name and the last signpost or junction you passed. Tell someone your plan before you go.
Acadia National Park21
On the trail

Wildlife and Leave No Trace

Acadia gets millions of visits on a small island. Little habits, repeated by everyone, keep it wild.

Wildlife

What you may see

  • Peregrine falcons on the cliffs (with binoculars)
  • Harbor seals, seabirds, sometimes whales from boat tours
  • White-tailed deer, red squirrels, snowshoe hares
  • Black bears live here but are rarely seen
  • If an animal changes what it is doing, you are too close
  • Never feed any animal, even gulls or chipmunks
Leave No Trace

Every visit

  • Stay on the trail and on bare rock, never on summit plants
  • Do not stack rocks or change cairns
  • Pack out all trash, peels and tissues
  • Leave rocks, shells, flowers and tide pool animals
  • No swimming or pets in public water supplies like Jordan Pond
  • Drones are not allowed in national parks
  • Found an old object? Leave it, photo it, tell a ranger

Ticks

Deer ticks live in Maine's woods and grass. Walk the middle of the trail, wear long pants in brush, use repellent, and check everyone (and the dog) every evening. Pull ticks out early with tweezers, and see a doctor if a rash or fever follows.

Mosquitoes and black flies

Worst in late May and June, especially near ponds and in still air. Bring repellent and a light long-sleeve shirt.

With dogs

Acadia is dog-friendly

  • Most trails and carriage roads allow leashed dogs
  • Leash 6 ft (1.8 m) or shorter, at all times
  • Pick up and pack out all waste
  • No dogs on rung and ladder trails (Beehive, Precipice and others)
  • No dogs in public water supplies like Jordan Pond
  • Beaches: limits in summer, for example Sand Beach May 15 to Sept 15

Kids near cliffs and water

Hold hands at every overlook without a railing. Set one rule before you start: nobody goes past the adults toward an edge or the waterline.

Dogs and heat

Granite heats up in the sun. Carry water and a bowl for the dog, rest in the shade, and check paws on rough rock.

Pet and beach rules change. Check "Pets" on nps.gov/acad before you go.
