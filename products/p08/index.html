How to use this guide

The Grand Canyon is a mile deep and up to 18 miles wide, and most visitors only see it from the top. This guide helps you plan a first trip to the South Rim: the side that is open all year, with the most viewpoints, walks and services.

What is inside

  • The park at a glance, and when to go
  • A booking timeline, fees and passes
  • Airports, gateway towns and the free shuttles
  • Sample 1, 2 and 3-day plans
  • 6 rim walks for every fitness level
  • 4 short canyon hikes with turnaround points
  • Heat, water, winter ice and edge safety
  • Packing list, day plans, budget and trip log

Who it is for

First-time visitors and beginner to intermediate hikers. Every walk here is a day walk on a marked trail, from a flat paved path to a steep half-day climb back out of the canyon.

How to read the hikes

Distances are round trip, in miles and kilometers. Elevation is in feet and meters. Inside the canyon you walk down first, so the climb is the part you do at the end, when you are tired. The figures come from the National Park Service.

Checked against nps.gov in October 2026

Fees, shuttle seasons, water stations and trail closures change, and floods, fires and storms can close areas at short notice. Before you go, check the official pages on page 27, and ask a ranger when you arrive.

Grand Canyon South Rim3
Timing

When to go

Spring and fall are the best seasons for a first hiking trip: mild days on the rim and a canyon that is warm, not dangerously hot. Summer is the busiest time, and the inner canyon is at its most dangerous.

March to May

Spring

Rim highs climb from the 50s and 60s °F (10 to 21 °C) in April toward the 70s and 80s by June. Nights can still freeze in April and May, and the upper trails can be icy early in spring.

June to August

Summer

Rim highs are usually in the 80s °F (27 to 32 °C), but the canyon floor passes 100 °F (38 °C) and can exceed 110 °F (43 °C). Thunderstorms are frequent in July and August. The busiest months.

September to November

Fall

Rim highs fall from the 60s °F (16 to 21 °C) in September to the 50s by November, and the canyon cools from the 90s toward the 70s. Storms ease after mid-September. Fewer people after Labor Day.

December to February

Winter

Rim highs in the 40s °F (4 to 9 °C), nights in the teens (about -10 °C). The South Rim gets about 58 in (147 cm) of snow a year. Quiet and beautiful, but trails can be icy (page 18).

Crowds

Mather Point, the Village and the South Entrance are busiest from late morning to mid-afternoon, on weekends and in summer. Come in early or late, use the free shuttles instead of hunting for parking, and do your canyon hike first thing in the morning.

Plan ahead

Booking timeline

Beds and campsites inside the park fill early for spring, summer and fall. Book where you sleep first, then plan the days around it. Day hikes need no permit.

15 months before

Phantom Ranch, at the bottom of the canyon, books by online lottery 15 months ahead. It is a hike or mule ride down, never a day trip. Closed after a flood in August 2026: check its status first.

As early as allowed

South Rim lodges: El Tovar, Bright Angel, Kachina, Thunderbird and Maswik (grandcanyonlodges.com), and Yavapai Lodge (visitgrandcanyon.com). They often fill a year ahead. Check each site for its booking window.

6 months before

Mather Campground (open all year) and Desert View Campground (spring to mid-fall) on recreation.gov. You can book up to six months ahead.

Any time

Rooms in Tusayan, Williams and Flagstaff, rental cars and flights. A backcountry permit (recreation.gov) is only needed to sleep below the rim.

On arrival

Nothing. There is no timed entry, and the shuttle buses need no tickets.

Getting there

Airports and gateway towns

Most visitors fly to Phoenix or Las Vegas and drive. Plan a car: the park shuttles are great once you are there, but there is little public transport to the park.

Two ways in

The South Entrance (Highway 64 through Tusayan) leads to Grand Canyon Village. The East Entrance (Highway 64 from Cameron, 25 mi / 40 km away) brings you in at Desert View. Come in one way and leave the other to see the whole rim drive.

Gateway towns

Tusayan, 7 mi (11 km) south. Hotels and restaurants right outside the South Entrance. Summer shuttle into the park.

Williams, 60 mi (100 km) south. Route 66 town and home of the Grand Canyon Railway, a historic train to the Village (book ahead).

Flagstaff, 80 mi (130 km) southeast. A real city with the most rooms, food and gear stores.

Inside the park. The least driving and the best sunrises, if you book far ahead (page 5).

Fill up before you arrive. Gas stations are few, and in winter some services at Desert View close. Keep the tank over half full.

Sample plans

1 day and 2 days

1 day: the essentialsSpring or fall
Early
Hikers' Express or the Orange shuttle to the South Kaibab Trailhead. Walk down to Ooh Aah Point (hike A) and back up while it is cool.
Morning
Orange shuttle to Mather Point, then walk the Rim Trail to Yavapai Point and the Geology Museum (walk 1).
Afternoon
Walk the Trail of Time toward the Village (walk 2). Lunch, then look around the historic buildings by El Tovar.
Evening
Red shuttle out Hermit Road to Hopi Point or Mohave Point for sunset (walk 3). The last bus leaves one hour after sunset.
2 days: rim and canyonArrive the evening before if you can
Day 1
Mather Point and the Rim Trail to Yavapai (walk 1), the Trail of Time (walk 2). Red shuttle to Hopi Point for sunset.
Day 2
Early start: Cedar Ridge on the South Kaibab (hike B) or the Bright Angel to the 1.5 Mile Resthouse (hike C). After lunch and a rest, ride the Red shuttle to Monument Creek Vista and walk the Greenway to Hermits Rest (walk 4).

How many days do you need?

One day shows you the famous views and a taste of the trail. Two days lets you hike below the rim without rushing. Three days adds the quiet east side and a sunrise. In summer, plan your hikes for the first hours after dawn and keep the middle of the day for the rim, the museum and shade.

Sample plans

3 days on the South Rim

3 days: slow and completeSleep in the park or Tusayan
Day 1
Arrive, park once and switch to the shuttles. Mather Point, the Rim Trail to Yavapai (walk 1) and the Trail of Time to the Village (walk 2). Sunset at Hopi Point.
Day 2
Canyon day. Start at first light on the South Kaibab: Cedar Ridge (hike B), or Skeleton Point only if it is cool and you are a strong hiker. Rest in the afternoon. Evening walk on the Greenway to Hermits Rest (walk 4).
Day 3
Sunrise at Mather Point or Yavapai Point. Drive east on Desert View Drive: Shoshone Point (walk 5), Grandview Point, Moran Point and Lipan Point, then the Watchtower at Desert View (walk 6). Leave by the East Entrance.

Make it your own

Have more time? Add the Bright Angel to the 3 Mile Resthouse (hike D) in the cool season, or ride the Grand Canyon Railway from Williams. Less energy? Every day here works on the rim alone, with no hike below the edge.

Rules of thumb for planning

  • One canyon hike a day, started early and finished before the heat
  • Use the shuttles: parking near the rim fills by late morning
  • Plan sunset and sunrise spots ahead, and check the last bus time
  • Keep one loose half-day for storms, closures or a slow morning
  • Write your plan on the day pages (23 to 24) and leave a copy with someone at home

Grand Canyon South Rim14
On the trail

Trail manners and practical stuff

The two main trails are busy, steep and shared with mules. A few simple habits keep everyone safe.

Always

Do

  • Give uphill hikers the right of way
  • Step to the uphill side for mules and stand still until the last one is 50 ft (15 m) past
  • Pack out every bit of trash, including food scraps
  • Stay on the trail: cutting switchbacks causes erosion and falls
Never

Don't

  • Feed the squirrels or birds, even a crumb
  • Throw rocks over the edge: people hike below you
  • Take a dog below the rim (only service animals, after checking in)
  • Count on finding water on the trail

Water and toilets on the trails

Both trailheads have drinking water all year: in 2026, from a large black tank beside the restrooms. On the Bright Angel, the resthouses normally have water from May to September only, and pipe breaks can shut it off without warning. The South Kaibab has no water at all. Toilets: Cedar Ridge, and the 1.5 and 3 Mile Resthouses.

Fall 2026: what changed

On August 29, 2026 a flash flood in Bright Angel Canyon damaged trails, bridges and the park's water line. The day hikes in this guide reopened, but Phantom Ranch, the river bridges and the North Kaibab Trail stayed closed, trail water was off for the winter, and South Rim lodges closed to overnight guests until November 20. Check nps.gov/grca/planyourvisit/key-messages.htm before your trip.

The golden rule

Down is optional, up is mandatory

That old Grand Canyon saying explains most rescues here. Walking down feels easy and fast, so people go too far. Then they have to climb back out in the heat of the day, tired and out of water.

1 hour
walking down

Feels like a gentle stroll.

2 hours
walking back up

The NPS says: plan on twice as long to hike up as it took to hike down.

1

Set a turnaround time

Before you start, decide when you will turn back, wherever you are. Then stick to it.

A simple test: turn around when one third of your water and energy is gone.
2

It gets hotter

The temperature rises about 5.5 °F for every 1,000 ft you go down (about 3 °C per 300 m).

The 3 Mile Resthouse is about 11 °F (6 °C) warmer than the rim.
3

Help is slow

The NPS says more than 300 people are rescued from the canyon each year, and rescue can be delayed by heat, storms and other calls.

Plan to get yourself out.

Why not to the river and back in one day?

The river is about 4,400 to 4,800 ft (1,340 to 1,460 m) below the South Rim trailheads, and the round trip is about 15 mi (24 km) or more of steep trail, ending with the hardest climb of the day. The NPS asks hikers not to try it, especially from May to September. In fall 2026 the bridges at the bottom were closed after the flood anyway.

Feeling sick, dizzy or wiped out?

Stop in the shade, rest, eat something salty and drink. The NPS notes that recovering can take an hour or longer. Then walk up slowly, with rests, and ask other hikers for help if you need it.

Grand Canyon South Rim16
Safety

Heat, water and salty snacks

From May to September the inner canyon can pass 110 °F (43 °C). The NPS asks summer hikers not to be below the rim between 10 am and 4 pm.

Hike in the cool hours

Start at first light and be back on the rim before 10 am, or go after 4 pm with a headlamp for the walk up. Rest often in the shade.

Drink when thirsty

Carry enough water for the whole hike, plus extra, and a way to treat water. Drink when you are thirsty, but do not force down huge amounts: too much water without food can be dangerous too.

Eat more than usual

The NPS suggests eating double your normal carbohydrates and salty foods when hiking in heat. Pack pretzels, salted nuts, crackers and an electrolyte drink.

Stay wet, stay cool

Where there is water, soak your hat, scarf or shirt. A wet layer in the dry air cools you down fast.

Heat exhaustion

Signs

Heavy sweating, headache, nausea, dizziness, weakness, pale skin.

Stop in the shade, cool down with water, drink and eat something salty. Rest until you feel normal.

Heat stroke

Signs

Confusion, strange behavior, very hot skin, collapse. Life-threatening.

Call 911 or send for help at once. Move to shade and cool the person with water and fanning.

Low salt (hyponatremia)

Signs

Nausea, vomiting, confusion, lots of clear urine, after drinking a lot without eating.

Stop drinking more water. Eat salty food. If confused or vomiting, get help.

Confusion is always an emergency. If someone is not making sense, do not let them walk on alone: call 911 or get a ranger.
Grand Canyon South Rim17
Safety

Winter on the rim

Snow on the red walls is one of the canyon's best sights, and the rim is quiet. It also brings ice, cold nights and short days.

1

Ice

The NPS warns the upper parts of the Bright Angel and South Kaibab can be extremely icy in winter and early spring, and rim paths can be icy too.

Wear over-the-shoe traction devices and use hiking poles.
2

Cold

Highs in the 40s °F (4 to 9 °C) and nights in the teens (about -10 °C) on the rim. Much warmer down in the canyon.

Dress in layers you can open on the climb and close at the top.
3

Short days

The sun sets around dinner time and canyon walls shade the trails early.

Carry a headlamp, and check sunset before you start.

Roads

Snow can close roads for a while, including Hermit Road and Desert View Drive. From December to February you can drive Hermit Road in your own car, since the shuttle does not run. For road conditions, the park lists (928) 638-7496.

What to pack

  • Traction devices and poles
  • Warm hat, gloves, puffy jacket
  • Waterproof boots and wool socks
  • Hot drink in an insulated bottle
  • Headlamp with spare batteries

Turn back at ice you cannot handle

If the trail looks like a frozen slide and you have no traction devices, enjoy the view from the top. A fall on an icy switchback above a drop is not worth any viewpoint.

Grand Canyon South Rim18
Safety

Altitude, storms and sun

The South Rim sits at 7,000 ft (2,135 m), higher than most visitors live. The air is thin and dry, the sun is strong and summer storms build fast.

1

Altitude

The NPS lists sunburn, dehydration, nausea, shortness of breath and exhaustion as risks up here. Take your time on the first day.

Headache or nausea that does not ease with rest? Take it easy and get help if it gets worse.
2

Storms

Thunderstorms are frequent in July, August and early September, usually in the afternoon. Lightning has killed and injured visitors here.

Move away from the rim and lone trees. Shelter in a car, bus or building.
3

Flash floods

Rain far away can send a flood down a side canyon without warning. Stay out of narrow drainages when storms are around.

Rushing water may be the first sign of rain upstream.

Sun

Many trails have little or no shade. Wear a wide-brim hat, sunglasses and sunscreen even on cool days, and a light long-sleeve shirt in summer.

Layers

Summer days in the 80s °F (27 to 32 °C) and nights in the 40s (4 to 9 °C) are normal. Carry a warm layer and a wind or rain jacket on every hike.

Check every morning

  • Forecast at weather.gov for Grand Canyon Village
  • Park alerts and the Key Hiking Messages page
  • Sunrise and sunset times
  • Heat warnings for the inner canyon
  • Air quality on airnow.gov if there is smoke
Grand Canyon South Rim19
Safety

The edge and the wildlife

Most rim viewpoints have railings, but long stretches of the Rim Trail do not. The NPS asks everyone to stay at least 6 ft (2 m) from the edge.

At the edge

Stay safe

  • Keep both feet on the path or the paved overlook
  • Never climb over railings or walls for a photo
  • No running, jumping or stunts near the rim
  • Hold children's hands at every viewpoint
  • Look where you step when you walk and take photos
Wildlife

Give room

  • Elk and bison: at least 100 ft (30 m)
  • Deer, bighorn sheep, condors, mountain lions: at least 75 ft (23 m), about two bus lengths
  • Squirrels, birds and reptiles: at least 20 ft (6 m)
  • Never feed any animal. It is illegal in all national parks

The most dangerous animal is the cute one

The NPS says rock squirrels cause the most injuries to visitors here. Fed by people, they lose their fear and bite, and they can carry disease. Keep food packed away at viewpoints.

Elk on the road

Elk often walk through the Village and along the roads, especially at dawn and dusk. They are most aggressive in the fall rut and in spring with calves. Watch from your car or from far away.

Pets

Leashed pets may walk the Rim Trail, but they are not allowed below the rim, in buildings or on the shuttle buses. A kennel on the South Rim boards pets during your hike.

For a rim day

Two climates in one hike

A cold, windy morning on the rim can turn into a hot afternoon a few thousand feet below. Start with layers you can take off, and keep a dry shirt and extra water in the car.

Ways to spend less

One car pass covers everyone in it for 7 days. The shuttles, rim walks, ranger talks and Junior Ranger program are free. With children

Kids at the canyon

Kids love the Grand Canyon: rocks to touch, ravens and condors to spot, and a badge to earn. The one rule that matters most is the edge.

Rim rules for families

Hold hands at every viewpoint. Stay on paved paths and behind railings, at least 6 ft (2 m) from the edge everywhere else. Agree on a meeting spot in case you get separated, and put your phone number in each child's pocket.

Junior Ranger program

Free, for ages 4 and up (adults can join too). Pick up a booklet at any visitor center, finish the activities for your age, and bring it back for a certificate and badge. Available all year on the South Rim.

Best walks with kids

  • Trail of Time: paved, flat, real rocks to touch (walk 2)
  • Yavapai Geology Museum and its giant windows
  • The Greenway to Hermits Rest: wide and paved (walk 4)
  • The Watchtower at Desert View (walk 6)

Below the rim with kids

Keep it short. Ooh Aah Point (hike A) is the best first taste for older kids who are steady on their feet. Remember that every step down is one they must climb back up, and children overheat faster than adults.

Pack for kids

  • Their own water bottle and salty snacks
  • Hats and sunscreen
  • A warm layer for cold mornings
  • Binoculars and a notebook for the Junior Ranger booklet
  • A small treat for the climb back up
Never let children run ahead on the Rim Trail, and never lift them onto walls or railings for a photo.
