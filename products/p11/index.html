How to use this guide

Rocky Mountain National Park is high country: most trailheads sit between 8,000 and 9,500 ft (2,400 to 2,900 m), and the main road crosses the Continental Divide above 12,000 ft. This guide helps you plan a first visit without the stress.

What is inside

  • Gateway towns, campgrounds and shuttles
  • Altitude, afternoon storms and wildlife

  • Trail Ridge Road and Old Fall River Road

Who it is for

First-time visitors and beginner to intermediate hikers. Every hike here is a day hike on a marked trail, from a flat lakeside loop to a half-day walk to a waterfall.

How to read the hikes

Distances are round trip, in miles and kilometers. Climbs are total elevation gain from the trailhead, in feet and meters. All trail figures come from the National Park Service.

Checked against nps.gov in November 2026

Timed entry dates, road openings, shuttles and fees change every year. Snow, storms and wildlife can close trails and roads at short notice. Before you go, check the official pages on page 26, and ask a ranger when you arrive.

The park

At a glance

The Continental Divide splits the park in two. The east side, near Estes Park, has most of the famous lakes and the busiest trails. The west side, near Grand Lake, is quieter, with wide river meadows and moose.

Trail Ridge Road joins the two sides

The highest continuous paved road in the US runs 48 mi (77 km) from Estes Park to Grand Lake and tops out at 12,183 ft (3,713 m). It is usually open from late May to mid or late fall, and closed in winter (page 15).

Three things that surprise first-timers

You need a timed entry reservation in summer and early fall (page 5). Even easy trails feel hard at this altitude on the first day (page 8). And there are no hotels inside the park: you sleep in a gateway town or a campground (page 7).

Rocky Mountain National Park3
Timing

When to go: month by month

For a first hiking trip, late June to September gives you open roads and snow-free trails. September is the favorite: golden aspens, bugling elk and fewer storms, but busy weekends.

Jan to MarDeep winter. Trail Ridge Road closed. Snowshoeing above about 9,000 ft. Few people, short days, strong winds up high.
AprilStill winter on the trails. Lower trails have patchy snow and ice. Good for wildlife watching in the meadows.
MaySnow melts low down; high trails stay snowy. Trail Ridge Road usually opens the last week of May. Timed entry starts late May.
JuneLakes thaw, waterfalls are full. Snow can linger on trails above 10,000 ft. Busy from mid-June.
JulyWildflowers on the tundra. The busiest month. Old Fall River Road usually opens early July. Afternoon storms most days.
AugustWarm days, busy trails, frequent afternoon thunderstorms. Start every hike early.
SeptemberElk rut begins, aspens turn gold mid to late month. Cooler, clear mornings. Weekends very busy.
OctoberElk rut continues, first snows up high. Timed entry ends mid-October. Old Fall River Road closes early October.
Nov to DecTrail Ridge Road closes for the winter (mid-November in 2025). Quiet, cold, icy trails: bring traction.

Snow lasts longer than you think

A warm sunny day in Estes Park in June can still mean snow and ice on the trail to Emerald Lake. Ask at a visitor center about trail conditions, and pack traction devices from October to June.

Rocky Mountain National Park4
Timed entry

Timed entry, explained

From late May to mid-October you need a timed entry reservation to drive into the park during the busiest hours. It is the part of the trip people most often get wrong, so plan it first.

Option 1

Timed Entry

9 am to 2 pm
Whole park except Bear Lake Road

For Trail Ridge Road, the west side, Wild Basin, Lumpy Ridge, Longs Peak and Lily Lake. In 2026: May 22 to October 12.

Option 2

Timed Entry+ Bear Lake Road

5 am to 6 pm
Bear Lake Road and the rest of the park

Needed for Bear Lake, Glacier Gorge, Sprague Lake and the other trailheads on Bear Lake Road. In 2026: May 22 to October 18.

How it works

Pick a 2-hour window

Each reservation lets you enter during a two-hour window. Once you are in, stay as long as you like: there is no set time to leave.

Book on recreation.gov

Online, in the Recreation.gov app or by phone. Not at the park gates or visitor centers. The only cost is a $2 processing fee, which is not refunded.

Monthly release

In 2026 each month opened at 8 am Mountain Time on the 1st of the month before: May 1 for late May and June, June 1 for July, July 1 for August, August 1 for September, September 1 for October.

Night-before release

More reservations are released at 7 pm Mountain Time the evening before each date. They go fast, so be logged in and ready.

Plus your pass

The reservation is not a ticket. You still need a park entrance pass or an annual pass (page 7).

Planning a 2027 trip?

This page describes the 2026 system. The park announces each year's dates and rules ahead of the season. Check nps.gov/romo and search "timed entry" before you book anything else.

If you miss out, and what to do on the day

Sold out for your date? You can still see the park. These are the legal, simple ways around a missing reservation.

1

Go early

No reservation is needed to enter the rest of the park before 9 am. Bear Lake Road needs Timed Entry+ from 5 am.

Early starts also beat the afternoon storms (page 9).
2

Go late

After 2 pm you can enter the rest of the park without a reservation. In 2026, a missed Bear Lake Road window meant coming back after 2 pm.

Evening is the best time to see elk in the meadows.
3

Try again

Check Recreation.gov at 7 pm the night before. Plans change and spots can open up.

Set a phone alarm for 6:55 pm Mountain Time.

Camping or backpacking?

Campground and wilderness permit holders have their own entry rules. Check the timed entry FAQ on nps.gov/romo, or call the park at (970) 586-1206.

Bear Lake and Glacier Gorge parking fills early on summer and fall days, even for people with a reservation. Arrive early or park at the Park & Ride and take the shuttle.
Rocky Mountain National Park6
Getting there

Getting there, staying, and paying

Most visitors fly into Denver and drive. Denver International Airport is about 76 mi (122 km) from the Beaver Meadows Visitor Center on the east side.

Estes Park

East side · most rooms

Hotels, cabins, restaurants and gear stores right outside the Beaver Meadows and Fall River entrances. Best base for Bear Lake Road.

Grand Lake

West side · quieter

A small lakeside town by the Grand Lake Entrance. When Trail Ridge Road is closed, the drive from Estes Park is about 145 mi (233 km) around the park.

Campgrounds in the park

There are no hotels or lodges inside the park. Moraine Park, Glacier Basin, Aspenglen and Timber Creek (west side) take reservations on Recreation.gov up to 6 months ahead. Longs Peak (tents only, about July to early September) is the only first-come, first-served campground. Moraine Park is the only one open in winter, first-come, first-served. Timber Creek closed in August 2026 for repairs: check its status.

Rocky Mountain National Park7
Safety

Altitude: why the first day feels hard

At Bear Lake there is noticeably less oxygen in each breath than at sea level. Even fit hikers puff on easy climbs here. That is normal. Plan around it.

1

Go slow

Choose an easy, short hike on day one (Bear Lake, Sprague Lake, Alberta Falls) and save the longer climbs for later in the trip.

Walk at a pace where you can still talk.
2

Drink and eat

The air is dry and you lose water fast. The park suggests several quarts (liters) of water a day. Eat regular snacks.

Go easy on alcohol the first night.
3

Sun is stronger

There is less atmosphere to block the sun up here, and severe sunburn is a real risk, even on cool days.

Reapply sunscreen often.

Mild altitude sickness

Signs

Headache, nausea, dizziness, tiredness, poor sleep.

Rest, drink water and do not go higher. If it does not get better, go down to a lower elevation.

Getting worse

Signs

Headache that does not ease, vomiting, getting more tired and clumsy.

Go down now, with someone. Even 1,000 to 2,000 ft (300 to 600 m) lower can help a lot.

Emergency

Signs

Confusion, stumbling, short of breath while resting, a wet cough.

Call 911 or get a ranger. Go down at once and get medical care.

Altitude can make existing health problems worse. If you have a heart or lung condition, or are pregnant, ask your doctor before a trip to high elevation. This page is general information, not medical advice.

An easy trick

Spend your first night in Estes Park or Grand Lake, not straight off a flight into a high trailhead. Your body adjusts a little each day you spend up high.

Rocky Mountain National Park8
Safety

Afternoon thunderstorms

In summer, clear mornings often turn into thunderstorms by early afternoon. Above treeline there is nowhere to hide from lightning, so the whole plan comes down to timing.

Start early
on the trail at first light

Most storms build after midday in July and August.

Be down early
below treeline by early afternoon

Aim to be off open ridges and summits by noon.

Watch the sky

  • Puffy clouds growing tall by late morning
  • Dark bases and a cold gust of wind
  • Distant thunder: you are already in range
  • Hair standing up or a buzzing sound: move now

If a storm catches you

  • Get off summits and ridges, go down
  • Move away from lone trees and big rocks
  • Avoid small caves and rock overhangs
  • If you cannot get lower, crouch low on your heels
  • Spread a group out, at least 50 ft (15 m) apart

Weather changes fast

The park warns that a sunny day can quickly turn windy and wet, with driving rain or even snow, in any month. Carry a rain jacket and a warm layer on every hike, even a short one. Hypothermia can happen in summer when you are wet and tired in the wind.

The safe car

A hard-top car is a good shelter from lightning. On Trail Ridge Road, if a storm rolls in, stay in your car and enjoy the view through the windshield.

Rocky Mountain National Park9
Safety

Wildlife: elk, moose and bears

Elk graze in the meadows by the road, moose feed in the west-side willows, and black bears live in the forests. Watching them is a highlight. Getting close is how people get hurt.

Keep your distance

The park's rules

  • Elk and bighorn sheep: at least 75 ft (23 m)
  • Moose, bears and mountain lions: at least 120 ft (37 m)
  • If an animal reacts to you, you are too close
  • Never feed any animal, even a chipmunk
  • Calling elk and spotlighting wildlife are banned
Where and when

Seeing them safely

  • Elk: meadows at dawn and dusk, all year
  • Bighorn sheep: Sheep Lakes and Horseshoe Park, May to mid-August
  • Moose: Kawuneeche Valley on the west side
  • Use binoculars or a zoom lens
  • Pull fully off the road to watch

Moose

Moose look calm but can charge, especially cows with calves. If one walks toward you, back away and put a tree or car between you.

Elk rut (September and October)

Bulls are aggressive. Some meadows close to people on foot from evening to mid-morning: Horseshoe Park, Upper Beaver Meadows, Moraine Park, Harbison Meadow and Holzwarth Meadow.

Black bears and mountain lions

Rarely seen. If you meet one, do not run. Stand tall, make noise, and back away slowly. Fight back if attacked.

Food storage

Never leave food, coolers or trash out at picnic areas or campsites. Use the food lockers in campgrounds, and keep food out of sight in a closed car.

Rocky Mountain National Park14
Scenic drives

Trail Ridge Road and Old Fall River Road

Trail Ridge Road crosses the park in 48 mi (77 km), with 11 mi (18 km) above 11,500 ft (3,500 m). Allow at least half a day with stops. In season, the standard Timed Entry covers it.

Many Parks Curve

Big view over the valleys of the east side. Winter road closure point.

Rainbow Curve

Wide overlook as you climb toward treeline.

Forest Canyon Overlook

Short paved path to a view down a deep glacial canyon.

Rock Cut

The Tundra Communities Trail (hike 9): 1 mi (1.6 km) round trip, 200 ft (61 m), above 12,000 ft (3,660 m).

Alpine Visitor Center

11,796 ft (3,595 m). Exhibits, restrooms, food. Summer only.

Milner Pass

The Continental Divide, with Poudre Lake beside the road.

Farview Curve

Looks down on the Kawuneeche Valley and the Never Summer Mountains.

Season

Usually opens to through travel in the last week of May and closes for winter in mid or late fall (November 14 in 2025). In winter, you can drive the east side up to Many Parks Curve and the west side up to the Colorado River Trailhead. Road status line: (970) 586-1222, 24 hours.

Old Fall River Road

An 11 mi (18 km), mostly gravel road with no guardrails, from Horseshoe Park up to the Alpine Visitor Center. One way, uphill only: you cannot drive it down. Speed limit 15 mph. Usually open to cars from early July to early October. No vehicles over 25 ft (7.6 m) and no trailers.

On the tundra, stay on the paths. Alpine plants grow very slowly, and one footstep can kill a plant that took years to grow. Up here it is often 20 °F (11 °C) or more colder than in Estes Park, and windy: bring a jacket.

Sample plans

1 day in the park

1 day: the best of the east sideTimed Entry+ Bear Lake Road
Before 8
Enter early. Park at Bear Lake if there is space, or at the Park & Ride and ride the shuttle.
Morning
Walk the Bear Lake Loop (hike 1), then Nymph and Dream Lakes, and on to Emerald Lake if you feel good (hike 2).
Midday
Shuttle to Glacier Gorge and the short walk to Alberta Falls (hike 3). Picnic lunch.
Afternoon
Drive to Sprague Lake for the flat loop, or rest at the Moraine Park Discovery Center if storms build.

Watch elk in Moraine Park or Horseshoe Park from the roadside pullouts as the light fades.

Only have a day and no Bear Lake Road permit?

Enter before 9 am and drive Trail Ridge Road to the Alpine Visitor Center, with the Tundra Communities Trail at Rock Cut (page 15). Head back down by early afternoon, then walk to Adams Falls if you continue to Grand Lake, or watch elk in Horseshoe Park on the way back to Estes Park.

Rules of thumb

  • One main hike a day, started early
  • Easy hikes first, while your body adjusts to the altitude
  • Be below treeline by early afternoon in summer
  • Book timed entry before anything else in season
  • Keep a rainy-day backup (page 20)

3 days in the park

3 days: east, south and westSleep 2 nights in Estes Park, 1 in Grand Lake
Day 1
Arrive and adjust. Bear Lake Loop and Sprague Lake in the afternoon, elk at dusk in Moraine Park. Pick up a park map and the Junior Ranger booklet at Beaver Meadows.

Pace yourself

Three days at altitude add up. Keep one afternoon free for rest, and if anyone in the group has a headache or poor sleep, swap the next hike for an easy lake walk.

Rocky Mountain National Park19
Plan B

Rainy-day and rest-day ideas

Storms, smoke or tired legs are part of every mountain trip. Keep one of these in your pocket.

Visitor centers

East and west

Beaver Meadows has a park film and a big relief map. The Moraine Park Discovery Center (summer) has nature exhibits and a meadow view from upstairs. Kawuneeche on the west side.

Wildlife from the car

Dawn and dusk

Drive slowly through Moraine Park, Horseshoe Park and the Kawuneeche Valley. Pull fully off the road and watch with binoculars.

Holzwarth Historic Site

West side

A flat walk to a 1920s dude ranch with original cabins. A gentle outing between showers.

Short, low walks

Under 1 mi

Sprague Lake, Coyote Valley or Copeland Falls. Low, close to the car, and easy to cut short if thunder starts.

Ranger programs

Free

Talks and walks run all summer, and some in winter. Check the schedule at any visitor center or on nps.gov/romo.

Estes Park or Grand Lake

Town day

Gear shops, cafes and a lakeside walk in Grand Lake. Good for buying the rain jacket or traction devices you forgot.

Lightning nearby? Do not shelter under trees or in the open at a viewpoint. Get into a hard-top car or a building and wait 30 minutes after the last thunder.

Wildfire smoke

On smoky days, check air quality on airnow.gov and choose short, easy walks or a drive instead of a hard climb.

Rocky Mountain National Park20
Off season

Fall and winter visits

From late fall to spring the park is quiet, snowy and beautiful. No timed entry is needed, but roads close, days are short and trails are icy.

What stays open

Bear Lake Road, open all year (traction rules may apply), the east side of Trail Ridge Road to Many Parks Curve and Hidden Valley, the west side to the Colorado River Trailhead, and the Beaver Meadows and Fall River visitor centers. Moraine Park Campground stays open, first-come, first-served.

Driving

When Colorado's traction law is on, cars need snow-rated tires with enough tread, or chains or other approved devices. Check the forecast and road status before you set out.

Snowshoe basics

  • Most trails above about 9,000 ft can be snowshoed when there is snow
  • Rent snowshoes in Estes Park or Grand Lake (not in the park)
  • Waterproof boots, gaiters and poles
  • Stay off steep snow slopes and away from cornices
  • Check the avalanche forecast and ask a ranger
  • Ice is thin near lake inlets and outlets: stay off

Hidden Valley

Sledding

The only place in the park where sledding is allowed, on Trail Ridge Road. Plastic sleds only, no metal runners, feet first. Snow can be thin, so check conditions first.

Ranger snowshoe walks

Mid-January to mid-March

Ranger-led walks and talks when snow allows. Some need a reservation. Check the park's winter programs page.

Short days, cold nights

Start by mid-morning, turn around early, and carry a headlamp, a warm hat, gloves and a puffy jacket. Traction devices are worth it on the packed, icy trails near Bear Lake.

Rocky Mountain National Park21
With children

Kids and families

Mountain lakes, waterfalls, elk by the road and a badge to earn. Keep hikes short, start early and let the kids set the pace at altitude.

Junior Ranger program

Free booklets at the visitor centers for different ages. Finish the activities, then go back to a ranger to be sworn in and get a badge. In summer, the Junior Ranger Headquarters at Hidden Valley has extra programs.

Best hikes with kids

  • Bear Lake Loop: flat, 0.5 mi (hike 1)
  • Sprague Lake: flat loop with a fishing pier
  • Nymph or Dream Lake (hike 2)
  • Alberta Falls (hike 3)
  • Copeland Falls in Wild Basin (hike 5)
  • Coyote Valley and Adams Falls on the west side

Altitude and kids

Children can feel the altitude too, and may not say so. Watch for crankiness, headache, tummy ache or unusual tiredness, and stop, rest and go lower if it does not pass.

Pack for kids

  • Their own water bottle and plenty of snacks
  • Sunscreen, sunglasses and a hat
  • A warm layer and a rain jacket, even in July
  • Binoculars for elk and marmots
  • A small treat for the walk back
Hold hands near fast streams and waterfalls. The water is ice-cold and rocks by the edge are slippery. Never let children climb on wet rocks for a photo.

Babies and toddlers

Carriers work better than strollers on most trails. The Coyote Valley Trail and the walk to Holzwarth are stroller friendly.

For everyone

Accessibility

You do not need to hike far to see the best of the park. These places have accessible paths, parking or facilities, according to the NPS.

Shuttles

Most park shuttles are wheelchair accessible, with ramps, room for two wheelchairs, and drivers who can help. Tell the driver where you need to get off.

Passes and loans

US citizens and residents with a permanent disability can get the free Access Pass. Free all-terrain wheelchairs can be reserved in Estes Park: ask a visitor center for details.

Plan for the altitude too

Thin air affects everyone, on foot or on wheels. Take breaks, drink often and keep a warm layer handy. At the Alpine Visitor Center (11,796 ft / 3,595 m) the toilets are vault toilets across the parking lot, and accessible parking is limited, so arrive early.

Details change. For the latest Access Guide, go to nps.gov/romo and search "accessibility", or call (970) 586-1206.

Rocky Mountain National Park23
Park rules

Leave No Trace and park rules

More than four million people visit the park in a busy year. Small habits keep the lakes clean and the wildlife wild.

Always

Do

  • Stay on the trail, and walk through mud, not around it
  • Pack out all trash, including food scraps and peels
  • Use restrooms at trailheads before you start
  • Give wildlife space (page 10)
  • Let uphill hikers keep their pace
Never

Don't

  • Pick flowers, take rocks or antlers
  • Walk on the tundra off the paths
  • Feed any animal, even a chipmunk
  • Build rock stacks or carve into trees
  • Play music out loud on the trail

Pets

Pets are not allowed on any park trail, tundra or meadow, or in visitor centers. Leashed pets (6 ft / 1.8 m leash at most) may go on roads, in parking lots, campgrounds and picnic areas. Do not leave a pet in a hot car. Service animals are allowed; emotional support animals are not service animals.

Drones

Launching or flying drones is not allowed in US national parks, including Rocky Mountain.

Water

Do not drink from lakes or streams without treating it: boil it, or use a filter that removes Giardia.

Fires

Fires only in the grates at campgrounds and picnic areas, and only when there is no fire ban. Check the current fire rules at a visitor center.

Checklist

Packing list for the park

For every hike (the Ten Essentials)

  • Map, plus the NPS App with offline maps
  • Headlamp with fresh batteries
  • Sun protection: hat, sunglasses, sunscreen
  • First aid kit, with blister care
  • Knife or multi-tool
  • Fire starter (for emergencies only)
  • Emergency shelter: a space blanket
  • Extra food
  • Extra water, plus a filter or tablets
  • Extra layer: warm top and rain jacket

For this park

  • Timed entry reservation (screenshot it)
  • Park pass and a card (no cash)
  • Traction devices (October to June)
  • Binoculars for elk, moose and bighorns

Layers for altitude

  • Moisture-wicking base layer (no cotton)
  • Fleece or light puffy jacket
  • Waterproof rain jacket with a hood
  • Hiking pants or zip-offs
  • Warm hat and light gloves, even in summer
  • Broken-in hiking shoes with good grip
  • Hiking socks, plus a dry spare pair

For the car

  • More water and snacks for the drive
  • Warm jacket for Trail Ridge Road
  • Phone charger and power bank
  • Printed maps and this guide
  • Small trash bag: pack out everything
  • Dry clothes and shoes for after

Dress like an onion

A cold start at 7 am, a warm climb at 10 am and a windy, wet lake at noon are all normal on the same hike. Start a little cold, take a layer off before you sweat, and put one back on when you stop.
