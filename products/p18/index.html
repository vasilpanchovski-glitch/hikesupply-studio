How to use this guide

Yosemite is two parks in one: the famous valley with its cliffs and waterfalls, and a high country of granite domes, meadows and lakes that is only open by car for part of the year. Most of the planning comes down to three questions: when you go, where you sleep, and which roads are open.

Who it is for

First-time visitors and beginner to intermediate hikers. Every hike here is a day hike on a marked trail, from a flat loop in a sequoia grove to the steep granite steps of the Mist Trail.

How to read the hikes

Distances are round trip, as the National Park Service gives them, in miles and kilometers. Climbs are elevation gain in feet and meters. Times are NPS estimates for an average hiker: add breaks.

Checked against nps.gov in November 2026

Yosemite changes its entry rules, road openings and permits from year to year. In 2026 there was no entry reservation; that may not be true in 2027. Before you book, and again before you go, check the official pages on page 30 and ask a ranger when you arrive.

Print it or keep it offline

Cell service is weak or missing on most trails and along much of the high roads. Save this PDF to your phone before you leave home, or print the pages you need. Pages 26 to 29 are made for printing.

Yosemite National Park2
The park

Yosemite at a glance

Yosemite covers a huge area of the Sierra Nevada. The valley sits in the middle of the west side, and each other area is a drive of an hour or more from it. Plan your days by area, not by sight.

Yosemite ValleyAbout 4,000 ft / 1,200 m

The famous one: El Capitan, Half Dome, Yosemite Falls, Bridalveil Fall, meadows and the Merced River. Most lodging, food and the free valley shuttle are here. Also the busiest place in the park.

Glacier PointAbout 7,200 ft / 2,200 m

A viewpoint high above the valley, at the end of Glacier Point Road (usually open May to November). Short hikes to Sentinel Dome and Taft Point.

Tuolumne MeadowsAbout 8,600 ft / 2,600 m

Wide subalpine meadows, domes and lakes along Tioga Road, open by car only in summer and fall. Cooler, quieter, and a lot higher: take it slower.

Mariposa GroveNear the South Entrance

The park's largest grove of giant sequoias, including the Grizzly Giant. A free shuttle runs from the parking area in season.

WawonaSouth side

A historic hotel, a meadow loop and the Chilnualna Falls trail. Close to the Mariposa Grove.

Hetch HetchyNorthwest corner

A reservoir in a granite valley with spring waterfalls and one of the longest hiking seasons in the park. Its entrance is not open 24 hours.

Three things that surprise first-timers

The drive from the park entrance to the valley takes 45 minutes to over an hour. Tioga Road and Glacier Point Road close for winter, so half the park is out of reach by car for months. And many famous waterfalls are a trickle or dry by late summer (page 4).

Timing

When to go: month by month

There is no single best month. Spring is for waterfalls, summer for the high country, fall for quiet. No one can promise when the high roads open: it depends on the winter's snow.

Jan, FebSnow on the valley rim, sometimes on the valley floor. Quiet trails, low prices. Tioga Road and most of Glacier Point Road closed. In February, people gather to watch Horsetail Fall glow at sunset: check nps.gov for any special rules that year.
March, AprilWaterfalls start to build as snow melts. Low trails open, high trails still snowy. Spring storms are common.
MayWaterfalls near their peak: Yosemite Falls usually peaks in May. Glacier Point Road and Tioga Road open when the snow is cleared (in 2026: May 9 and May 15). Memorial Day weekend is very busy.
JuneBig waterfalls, warm valley days, the high country waking up. Busy, especially on weekends.
July, AugPeak season. Hot in the valley, the best time for Tuolumne Meadows and its lakes. Yosemite Falls often slows to a trickle or dries by August. Wildfire smoke possible.
SeptemberFewer people after Labor Day, warm days, cool nights. Most high trails open. Low waterfalls.
OctoberFall color in the valley, cold mornings. Half Dome cables usually come down after the second Monday. First snow can close the high roads at any time.
Nov, DecTioga and Glacier Point roads close for the season, depending on snow. The valley stays open all year. Carry tire chains: chain controls can be in effect.

Entry

Getting in: passes and reservations

In several recent summers Yosemite asked visitors to reserve entry on busy days. For 2026 the park dropped that system. You only needed a park pass to drive in.

In 2026

No timed entry reservation. Instead the park managed traffic on busy days: rangers at crowded junctions, parking control in the valley, and alerts. The park asked visitors to buy a digital pass in advance, come midweek, arrive early or late, and explore beyond the valley. Saturday mornings were the worst for traffic.

Planning a 2027 trip?

Do not assume 2027 will work like 2026. The park announces its summer rules each winter or spring. Check nps.gov/yose and search "entrance reservations" before you book anything else.

Yosemite National Park5
Getting there

Getting there and getting around

There are five entrances. Pick the one closest to your first night's bed. Drive times from the cities vary a lot with traffic and road work: check your route the day before.

South EntranceHwy 41

From Fresno, Oakhurst and Fish Camp. Closest to the Mariposa Grove and Wawona.

Arch RockHwy 140

From Merced, Mariposa and El Portal. Lowest road into the valley, often the easiest in winter.

Big Oak FlatHwy 120 west

From the San Francisco Bay Area via Groveland. Leads to the valley and to Tioga Road.

Tioga PassHwy 120 east

From Lee Vining and the eastern Sierra. Summer and fall only, at 9,945 ft (3,031 m).

Hetch HetchyEvergreen Road

From Hwy 120 near Groveland. The only entrance not open 24 hours.

Valley shuttle

Free, all year, with stops at lodges, campgrounds and trailheads around Yosemite Valley. Park once and ride.

Mariposa Grove shuttle

Free, from the parking area near the South Entrance to the grove, from late April to November when conditions allow.

Tuolumne shuttle

Summer only, along Tioga Road between Tuolumne Meadows and Olmsted Point (in 2026 it ended September 13).

Glacier Point Tour

No public shuttle goes to Glacier Point. A ticketed tour bus runs in summer, reservations required.

YARTS buses

Regional buses into the valley from gateway towns, and from Mammoth Lakes over Tioga Pass in summer. Fares apply.

Valley parking

On busy days valley lots fill by mid-morning. Arrive before 9 am, park once at your lodging or a day-use lot, and use the shuttle and your feet for the rest of the day. There is no gas in the valley: fill up before you enter.

Yosemite National Park6
Planning

Where to stay, and when to book

Beds inside the park are few for the number of visitors. Book the bed first, then build the days around it.

Lodging in the park

Hotels, lodge rooms, cabins and tent cabins in Yosemite Valley (The Ahwahnee, Yosemite Valley Lodge, Curry Village) and the historic Wawona Hotel, run by the park's lodging concessioner. Links on nps.gov/yose. Summer dates go early.

Gateway towns

Oakhurst and Fish Camp (south), Mariposa and El Portal (west, Hwy 140), Groveland (Hwy 120 west), Lee Vining (east, summer). Count the drive time to the valley before you book.

Campgrounds, 5 months ahead

Upper, Lower and North Pines, Wawona and Hodgdon Meadow: released on the 15th of each month at 7 am Pacific, five months ahead. They sell out within minutes.

Campgrounds, closer in

Tuolumne Meadows: half the sites 2 months ahead on the 15th, half 2 weeks ahead. Crane Flat, White Wolf, Bridalveil Creek and others: 2 weeks ahead, rolling. Camp 4: 1 week ahead in season.

How to book

Recreation.gov or 877-444-6777. Make an account, set your clock, and be logged in before 7 am Pacific. Two reservations per visit.

No luck?

Check for cancellations daily, try a first-come campground in winter (Camp 4, Wawona, Hodgdon Meadow), or stay in a gateway town.

Rule of thumb

Bed first, rental car second, permits third (page 8). Sleeping in a vehicle is only allowed in a campsite you have reserved. Write every booking on page 28.

Permits

Half Dome and wilderness permits

You do not need a permit for any day hike in this guide. You need one for two things: climbing the Half Dome cables, and any night in the wilderness.

Day hike

Half Dome

Lottery
Every day the cables are up

The cables are usually up from the Friday before Memorial Day to the day after the second Monday in October. Up to 300 people a day may go past the base of the subdome.

Overnight

Wilderness

Permit
Any night outside a campground

Every overnight trip in the wilderness needs a permit, and a bear-resistant food canister. Backpackers to Half Dome get it on their wilderness permit.

Half Dome lotteries, in plain words

LotteryHow it works (2026 rules)
PreseasonApply March 1 to 31 on Recreation.gov for up to 6 people, with a list of dates. Results by email in mid-April. About 225 day-hike permits a day go this way.
DailyApply two days before your hike, from midnight to 4 pm Pacific. Results the same night. Good for flexible trips.
Cost$10 per application, plus $10 per person if you win. Bring photo ID; the permit holder must be with the group at the checkpoint.

Wilderness permits

60 percent of permits are given by a weekly lottery, opening 24 weeks before the start date. The other 40 percent open on Recreation.gov 7 days before. Start by choosing a trailhead: each one has a daily limit.

Half Dome is a long, hard day with a steep cable climb (page 22). Winning the lottery is not a reason to go: decide first if it is right for your group.

Safety

Rivers, waterfalls and the weather

Yosemite's water is its beauty and its biggest danger. The park warns that rivers and creeks have very strong currents, and that rock near water is slick even when it looks dry.

1

Stay out of fast water

Never swim or wade above a waterfall. Swimming at Emerald Pool and the Silver Apron, above Vernal Fall, is illegal and dangerous.

Spring melt makes rivers fast and ice cold.
2

Stay on the trail

Do not climb past railings or walk on wet rock by falls and cliffs, even for a photo.

Keep children within reach at overlooks.
3

Get off domes in storms

Summer afternoon thunderstorms are common. Leave domes, ridges and Half Dome before they build.

Thunder heard? Turn around.

Heat

  • Valley and Hetch Hetchy trails get hot in summer
  • Start steep hikes early, in the shade
  • Drink often and eat salty snacks
  • Smoky day? Check airnow.gov and pick a shorter hike

Altitude

Tuolumne Meadows is about 8,600 ft (2,600 m), Tioga Pass 9,945 ft (3,031 m). Coming up from the coast in one day, many people feel short of breath.

  • Go slower and drink water
  • Do a short hike on day one up high
  • If you feel ill and it gets worse, go down and get help
Emergency: call 911. Signal is patchy, so tell someone your plan before you go, and turn back early rather than late.

Safety

Bears and food storage

Yosemite's bears are black bears, even when their fur is brown or blond. They have learned that people mean food, and they open cars to get it. The rules here protect the bears as much as your trip.

50 yards
46 m, outside developed areas

Watch from a distance. Never approach.

$5,000
fine, at most

Plus your food or car can be impounded.

Food storage

Where food goes

  • Car: only in daylight, out of sight, windows fully closed
  • After dark: in the food locker, never in the car or trunk
  • Campsite or tent cabin: in the locker, latched
  • Hotel room: inside, doors and windows closed
  • Hiking or picnicking: within arm's reach, never left alone
If you see a bear

Know what to do

  • On a trail: keep your distance and let it move on
  • In a campground or parking lot, or if it comes toward you: yell loudly, wave, make yourself big
  • Never run from a bear
  • Never feed one, even by accident
  • Tell a ranger where you saw it

What counts as food

Anything with a smell: drinks, canned food, sunscreen, toothpaste, soap, trash, an empty cooler, unwashed dishes, baby wipes.

Backpacking

Bear-resistant canisters are required for overnight trips in the wilderness. Hanging food is no longer allowed.

Summary of the park's bear page. Read the full rules on nps.gov/yose before your trip.

Yosemite National Park10
On the trail

Leave No Trace and trail etiquette

Millions of people visit a small valley each year. Little habits, repeated by everyone, keep it the place you came to see.

Leave No Trace

Every visit

  • Stay on the trail, even when it is muddy or snowy
  • Do not cut switchbacks
  • Pack out all trash, peels and tissues
  • Leave rocks, cones, flowers and wood where they are
  • Never feed any animal, even a squirrel or a jay
  • Use restrooms; if none, go 200 ft (60 m) from water and trails
  • No drones: they are not allowed in national parks
Etiquette

On busy trails

  • Uphill hikers have the right of way
  • Step aside for horses and mules, on the downhill side
  • Pass with a friendly "on your left"
  • Keep music in headphones
  • Do not block steps and viewpoints for long photos
  • Pets: only on paved paths and roads, on a 6 ft leash, and the Wawona Meadow Loop

Sequoias need space

In the Mariposa Grove, stay on the trails and boardwalks. Giant sequoias have shallow roots, and feet packing the soil around them harm the trees.

Water on the trail

Treat any water from streams or lakes before you drink it: boil it, filter it or use tablets. Bring enough from the trailhead, because many trails have none.

Choose your hike

How to choose a hike

The best hike is the one your whole group finishes smiling. Pick by effort first, then by view.

LevelWhat it means hereExamples
EasyUnder 3 mi (5 km), little climb. Most families, most fitness levels.Lower Yosemite Fall, Cook's Meadow, Big Trees Loop
Moderate2 to 5 mi (3 to 8 km) with a real climb of 300 to 1,000 ft (90 to 300 m).Vernal Fall footbridge, Sentinel Dome, Lembert Dome
HardSteep and long, 1,000 ft (300 m) or more of climb, half a day or more.Top of Vernal and Nevada Falls, Cathedral Lakes
Very hardA full day, big climbs, permits or exposure.Upper Yosemite Fall, Half Dome (page 22)

How long will it take?

A simple planning rule many hikers use: about 2 mi (3 km) an hour on flat trail, plus about an hour for every 1,000 ft (300 m) of climb. Then add breaks, photos and lunch. The NPS times on each hike card already include a normal pace.

Worked example

Top of Vernal Fall: 2.4 mi (3.9 km) and 1,000 ft (300 m) of climb. That is about 1.2 hours of walking plus 1 hour for the climb, so 2 to 2.5 hours, plus breaks. The NPS says about 3 hours. Plan for 3.

Choose your hike

Water, start time and turnaround

Water

More than you think

Most Yosemite trails have no drinking water. The park recommends about 4 quarts (4 liters) for the top of Yosemite Falls in summer. For short walks, a liter per person is a minimum.

Start early

Before 8 am in summer

Cooler air, easier parking, and you are off domes before afternoon storms build.

Turnaround time

Write it down

Decide before you start when you will turn back, wherever you are. The way down on steep granite steps is slow, and it is where knees and ankles get hurt.

Footwear

Grip matters

Granite, sand and wet steps are slippery. Wear shoes with a good tread. Poles help a lot on the way down.

Turnaround planner

HikeStart timeTurn back byBack at car by

Turn back if

  • Anyone in the group is struggling, dizzy or very tired
  • You reach your turnaround time, even near the top
  • Clouds build fast or you hear thunder
  • The trail is covered in ice or water is running over it

The halfway rule

Use half your time and half your energy going out. The return on steep trails is just as long, and the light does not wait.

Upper Yosemite Fall

Hot, steep, no shade

The park recommends about 4 quarts (4 liters) of water. No water or restrooms on the trail. Start at first light in summer.

Half Dome

Permit, cables, exposure

The last 400 ft (120 m) is a cable climb up smooth granite. Never go up when the rock is wet or storms are near. Turning around below the top is a good choice, not a failure.

One-way trails

Need a ride up

Four Mile and Panorama run from Glacier Point down to the valley. Plan the ride up first (the Glacier Point Tour bus, reservations required).

Chilnualna Falls

Long and steep

The lower cascades are only 0.25 mi (400 m) from the trailhead. The rest is a long climb: turn back whenever you like.

Hikes this big go wrong in the afternoon: heat, storms, empty water bottles and tired legs on the way down. Set your turnaround before you start and keep it.

Valley, rim and high country5 nights

Mist Trail early (hikes 4 and 5). Easy afternoon.

Book for this plan

  • 3 nights near the valley
  • 2 nights at Tuolumne Meadows Campground or in Lee Vining
  • One 7-day vehicle pass

Altitude on day 4

You climb from about 4,000 ft (1,200 m) to 8,600 ft (2,600 m) in one drive. Keep day 4 easy, drink water, and save the long hike for day 5.

Before late May or after early November?

Tioga Road is usually closed. Swap days 4 and 5 for Hetch Hetchy (hike 11) and a quiet valley day: the Valley Loop Trail or Mirror Lake (hike 3).

Fuel for Tioga Road

Services in Tuolumne are very limited. The nearest gas is at Crane Flat inside the park, or Lee Vining outside it. Fill up and bring all the food and water you need for the day.

Packing checklist by season

Every hike (the Ten Essentials)

  • Map, plus the NPS App with offline maps
  • Headlamp with fresh batteries
  • Sun protection: hat, sunglasses, sunscreen
  • First aid kit, with blister care
  • Knife or multi-tool
  • Fire starter (for emergencies only)
  • Emergency shelter: a space blanket
  • Extra food
  • Extra water, plus a filter or tablets
  • Extra layer: warm top and rain jacket

For this park

  • Park pass on your phone (screenshot)
  • A card: the park is cashless
  • Shoes with good grip for wet granite
  • Trekking poles for steep steps
  • Small bag to pack out all trash

Spring (March to May)

  • Rain jacket for waterfall spray
  • Warm layer for cold mornings
  • Traction for icy trails early in spring

Summer (June to August)

  • Extra water: 3 to 4 liters for big days
  • Sun hat and a light long-sleeve shirt
  • Insect repellent for meadows
  • Warm layer for Tuolumne evenings

Fall (September to November)

  • Layers for freezing mornings
  • Hat and gloves up high
  • Headlamp: days are short

Winter (December to February)

  • Tire chains in the car
  • Insulated jacket, hat, gloves
  • Microspikes for icy trails
  • Thermos with a warm drink

Layers beat one big jacket

A morning in the valley and an afternoon at Glacier Point or Tuolumne can feel like two different seasons. Pack a base layer, a warm layer and a rain shell, skip cotton, and add or remove layers before you get too hot or too cold.
