How to use this tracker

One page per eight parks, in alphabetical order, so you can find any park in seconds. Tick it off, add the date, and press your passport stamp or a sticker into the box.

1

Print

Print at Actual size on US Letter. An A4 file and a low-ink printer-friendly file are included. Card stock holds stamps and glue best. Reprint any page for your own use as often as you like.

2

Check off

Page 3 lists all 63 parks on one sheet. Tick each park as you visit, then fill in its full card on pages 4 to 11.

3

Stamp

Most visitor centers have a free cancellation stamp for park passport books. Ink it right into the stamp box, or add a ticket stub or sticker.

4

Log

Use the visit log on pages 18 to 21 for the full story, and the two blank pages at the back for extra stamps, sketches and photos.

How to read a park card

On the cardWhat it means
BestWhen the park is usually most pleasant for day hiking. Every season can work with the right plan.
HikeOne short, well-known day hike from the park's own trail pages, with distance and elevation gain as NPS lists them, converted to km and m.
Round trip, loopRound trip means out and back the same way. A loop brings you back without repeating the trail.
LevelEasy, moderate or harder, as the park describes it. A few parks have no truly easy trail, and we say so.
NR FEEOne of 11 parks that charge an extra $100 per person (age 16+) to non-US residents in 2026, unless they hold the $250 non-resident annual pass. See page 16.
Rules change every year. Reservations, road openings and trail closures in this tracker were checked against nps.gov in October 2026. Always check the park's alerts page on nps.gov before you go. Page 13 shows how in two minutes.

Farthest from the mainland

The two territory parks

Many park lists leave these two out. They count, they are worth the trip, and they need a little more planning than a mainland park.

National Park of American Samoa

The only US national park south of the equator, spread over the islands of Tutuila, Ofu, Olosega and Taʻū: rainforest ridges, steep sea cliffs and coral reefs.

Getting there. Fly to Pago Pago on Tutuila. Flights are few, so plan the whole trip around them.

Village land. Much of the park is land the National Park Service leases from local villages. Be a polite guest: ask before you walk through a village, dress modestly, and ask at the visitor center about Sunday and evening customs.

Trails. NPS calls the park's trails unimproved, steep and rugged. Start with Lower Sauma Ridge (0.4 mi / 0.6 km round trip). Hike early, before the heat.

Virgin Islands National Park

Covers a large part of the island of St. John: white beaches, coral bays, old sugar plantation ruins and dry forest trails.

Getting there. Fly to St. Thomas, then take the ferry to St. John. There is no airport on St. John.

Papers. It is a US territory. Canadian and other visitors should bring a passport, as for any US trip.

Trails. Start with the Cinnamon Bay Nature Loop (about 0.5 mi / 0.8 km). The dry season, roughly December to April, is the most comfortable. Atlantic hurricane season runs June 1 to November 30.

Beach fee. Trunk Bay charges an amenity fee.

Before every trip

Checked against nps.gov in October 2026

Every park, trail, distance and entry note in this tracker was checked against the official National Park Service pages in October 2026. Parks change their rules often, so here is how to check the latest in two minutes.

1

Open the park page

Search "nps.gov" plus the park name. Any alert sits in a banner at the top of every page: closures, fire, flooding, road work.

2

Check your trail

Look under Plan Your Visit, then Conditions or Trail Closures. Trails close for storms, rebuilding and wildlife.

3

Check reservations

Timed entry, sunrise permits, cave tours and ferries are booked on recreation.gov or with the park's listed partner. Many open weeks or months ahead.

4

Ask a ranger

Call the visitor center or stop by when you arrive. Rangers know today's trail, weather and wildlife conditions better than any app.

Save on entry

Park passes in plain words

Prices as listed on nps.gov and parks.canada.ca in October 2026. They change, so check before you buy.

America the Beautiful annual pass

$80 US residents
$250 non-residents

Covers entrance fees at all US national parks, and many other federal sites, for 12 months. It covers the pass holder and the passengers in one private car.

nps.gov/planyourvisit/passes.htm

Non-resident fee, new for 2026

$100 per person, age 16+

Charged on top of the entrance fee at Acadia, Bryce Canyon, Everglades, Glacier, Grand Canyon, Grand Teton, Rocky Mountain, Sequoia and Kings Canyon, Yellowstone, Yosemite and Zion. The $250 non-resident annual pass covers it.

Other US passes

Senior (US citizens and residents 62+): $20 a year or $80 for life.

4th Grade: free for US 4th graders and their family car.

Military and Access (permanent disability): free.

Some parks charge no entrance fee at all, and the Great Smoky Mountains parking tag is separate from any pass.

Parks Canada Discovery Pass

CAD $83.50 adult, $71.50 senior
$167.50 family or group

12 months of entry to more than 80 Parks Canada places. Youth 17 and under enter free. The family or group pass covers up to 7 people in one vehicle.

parks.canada.ca/passes. Canada has run free-entry periods (the Canada Strong Pass) in 2025 and 2026. Watch the Parks Canada site for the next one.

Trail etiquette and Leave No Trace

Popular trails see a lot of boots. These seven habits keep them wild for the next hiker.

1

Plan ahead and prepare

Know the rules, the weather and your turnaround time. Carry water, food, layers, a light and a map.

2

Travel on durable surfaces

Stay on the trail, even through mud, so it does not widen. Never shortcut switchbacks.

3

Dispose of waste properly

Pack out everything you bring, food scraps and wrappers too. Use restrooms where there are some.

4

Leave what you find

Rocks, flowers, antlers and artifacts stay. Collecting is not allowed in national parks.

5

Minimize campfire impacts

Use a stove. Make fires only where allowed, in existing rings, and only when there is no fire ban.

6

Respect wildlife

Watch from a distance and never feed animals. Yellowstone asks for at least 100 yd / 91 m from bears and wolves.

7

Be considerate of others

Hikers going uphill have the right of way. Step aside for horses, keep music off, and let the quiet win.

Park-specific rules matter

Pets, drones and stamp hunting have their own rules in each park. Pets are only allowed on some trails, and launching a drone is not allowed in national parks without a permit. Check the park page before you go.

Pack these on every park day hike

  • Water, more than you think
  • Snacks or lunch
  • Rain shell and a warm layer
  • Map, offline or on paper
  • Headlamp or flashlight
  • Sun hat and sunscreen
  • Small first aid kit
  • Tell someone your plan and return time

Based on the seven Leave No Trace principles. Learn more at lnt.org.
