How to use this gift set

The best gift for a hiker is a day outside together. This set turns that promise into something you can wrap, then helps you plan the hike.

1

Print

Print the pages you want at actual size (100%). Card stock (65 to 110 lb, about 175 to 300 gsm) feels like a real gift; plain paper works too. Everything reads well in black and white, and there is a low-ink version in your download.

2

Choose and cut

Pick the coupons that suit them: 24 classic, 8 blank for your own ideas, 6 for kids and families and 6 for winter. Cut along the dashed lines with scissors or a paper trimmer.

3

Write

Fill in To, From and a Valid until date. A date makes the gift happen. Use the blank coupons for the hike only the two of you would understand.

4

Wrap

Fold the envelope on page 15, make a little booklet (page 19), add a gift tag from page 16, or tuck a Trail Pass inside a card.

Pack list

What to bring on the gift hike

The Ten Essentials, in plain words. The National Park Service recommends carrying them on every hike, even a short one.

1

Navigation

A map of the trail (paper or downloaded), and a phone or GPS. Know the route before you go.

2

Headlamp

Even on a day hike. Days are short in fall and winter, and delays happen. Spare batteries.

3

Sun protection

Sunglasses, sunscreen and a hat, in every season. Snow reflects sunlight.

4

First aid kit

Blister care, bandages and any personal medicines you need.

5

Knife or multi-tool

Plus a little repair tape for gear and straps.

6

Fire

Matches or a lighter for emergencies only. Follow local fire rules.

7

Emergency shelter

A light emergency blanket or bivy, in case you need to wait for help.

8

Extra food

More than you think you need. Easy snacks you both like.

9

Extra water

Enough for the whole hike, more on warm days, and a way to treat water.

10

Extra clothes

A warm layer and a rain or wind jacket, even if the morning is mild.

Cool and cold days

  • Base layer that is not cotton
  • Fleece or light puffy jacket
  • Warm hat and gloves
  • Warm drink in an insulated bottle

Warm days

  • Breathable shirt and sun hat
  • Extra water and salty snacks
  • Insect repellent
  • An early start to avoid the heat

First hike together?

Beginner-friendly tips

The goal is a great day that makes both of you want to go again. These simple habits help.

Start short

Pick an easy first trail

Choose a distance and climb the less experienced hiker is comfortable with. You can always pick a longer trail next time.

Pace

Walk at the slower pace

Let the slower hiker set the pace, ideally at the front. If you can chat while walking, the pace is right.

Daylight

Know when it gets dark

Check the sunset time, set a turnaround time and carry headlamps. In fall and winter, start earlier than feels necessary.

Conditions

Check official sources

Trails close for weather, fire, wildlife and repairs. Check the park website (NPS, Parks Canada or state and provincial parks) or ask a ranger.

Feet

Wear shoes you know

Use broken-in shoes and wool or synthetic socks. Stop and fix a hot spot as soon as you feel it, before it becomes a blister.

Breaks

Eat and drink often

Small snacks and sips every hour keep energy steady. Plan a fun stop: a view, a creek, a bench.

Leave No Trace

Stay on the trail, pack out everything you bring (food scraps too), leave rocks, flowers and pine cones where you find them, and give wildlife plenty of space.

Tell someone at home where you are going and when you will be back. Turn around if the weather changes, the trail looks unsafe or one of you is not feeling well. The trail will still be there next time.

Hike Together Gift Coupons18
Put it together

Print-and-assemble guide

Four easy ways to turn the cut coupons into a gift. All you need is what is already in the drawer.

1

Envelope

  1. Print page 15 on regular paper or thin card.
  2. Cut on the solid line and fold the flaps back on the dotted lines.
  3. Glue the bottom flap onto the side flaps.
  4. Add the coupons and close with a gift tag.
2

Stapled booklet

  1. Stack the coupons, with your favorite one on top.
  2. Line up the left edges.
  3. Staple twice on the left, about half an inch (1 cm) from the edge.
3

Ring booklet

  1. Punch a hole in the top left corner of each coupon.
  2. Thread them on a binder ring or a small carabiner-style key ring.
  3. Clip it to a daypack or water bottle as the gift.
4

Ribbon booklet

  1. Punch two holes along the left edge of each coupon.
  2. Thread a ribbon or a spare boot lace through.
  3. Tie a bow and add a gift tag.

Ideas for wrapping

  • Roll the coupons inside a new pair of hiking socks
  • Tuck them in a mug with hot chocolate mix
  • Put them in the lid pocket of a daypack
  • Wrap a Trail Pass inside a paper trail map
  • Tie them to a water bottle with ribbon
  • Hide one coupon in each stocking or advent day

Printing tips

Print at actual size (100%) and do a test page on plain paper first. US Letter and A4 versions are both in your download. For card stock, use your printer's thick paper setting and feed one sheet at a time if it jams.
