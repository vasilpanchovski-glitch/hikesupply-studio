3 mistakes that end most first hikes
Most first hikes that go wrong don't fail because of fitness. They fail because of a few small planning gaps. Here are the three we see most, and the easy fix for each.
1. Starting too late
Daylight runs out faster than you think, especially in autumn and winter. Check sunset time before you go, set a turnaround time, and head back when you reach it, even if you haven't made the top.
2. Wearing cotton
Cotton soaks up sweat and rain and stays wet, which can chill you fast when the wind picks up. Wear synthetic or wool layers and pack a light jacket, even on a sunny morning.
3. Not telling anyone your plan
Before you leave, tell someone where you're going, which trail you'll take and when you expect to be back. Cell service on trails is often patchy, so this simple step matters.
